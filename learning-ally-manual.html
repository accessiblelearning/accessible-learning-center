First-time learner: how to follow a task

Read the task title and its starting location before pressing anything. A keyboard command acts on the active application and focused control. Merely having these instructions visible does not make the other application active.

  1. Keep these instructions available. Open the application separately. On Windows, hold Alt, tap Tab to choose its window, and release Alt. Check the window title before continuing.
  2. Focus is the place receiving your next keystroke. A screen reader announces its name and role; a visible border or highlight may mark it. Tab selects the next keyboard-accessible control. Shift+Tab reverses that movement. Neither command means “activate.”
  3. At a button or link, press Enter to activate. A checkbox is an on/off choice: Space changes it. Listen for checked or not checked. In an open menu, use arrows to select an item, then Enter. Escape usually closes that menu; it does not universally return to the previous page.
  4. At a text field, enter only the requested information. Ctrl+A selects all text in that field; typing then replaces it. Confirm focus before doing this. Use Left and Right to review a small correction, or Backspace to remove the character before the cursor.
  5. After opening a dialog, listen to its title, read its instructions, and Tab through its controls. Use Cancel if you are unsure. Do not repeatedly activate a button while a page or file is loading.
  6. At the result checkpoint, stop. Say what changed. If the result is different, use that task’s recovery paragraph before attempting the next task.

With a screen reader, arrow-key reading and text editing can use different modes. If letters become navigation commands instead of text, confirm you reached an editable field and consult your reader’s forms/focus-mode instructions in the Screen Readers manual. Do not turn the reader off to solve an unidentified focus problem.

When a task says to locate a named control, start with Tab or Shift+Tab and listen to each complete label and role. Tab skips ordinary paragraphs. If the page itself needs to be read, use your screen reader’s reading commands. If you pass the same controls twice without finding the requested one, stop and check the application, page, account, and open dialogs. A fixed number of Tab presses is unreliable because layouts change.

Recording progress: Write down the task, your starting location, the result, and one step to practice again. These activities are optional and repeatable; they are not a sequence of locked lessons.

Before you begin

Choose one practice task at a time. Have your usual accessibility tools running and a way to record which steps helped. Use a familiar, non-sensitive document or book for the first exercise.

Keyboard directions

A plus sign means hold the first key while tapping the final key, then release both. For three-key combinations, hold both modifiers while tapping the last key. Do not type the plus sign. “Then” means release the first command before pressing the next key.

Tab moves forward through controls; Shift+Tab moves backward. Enter activates a focused link or button; Space commonly operates buttons and checkboxes. Arrow keys move within menus and lists. Pause after each step to check where focus went. For screen-reader reading and interaction modes, see the Screen Readers manual. Phone gestures are covered in the iPhone and iPad VoiceOver and Android TalkBack manuals.

Reading-service terms explained

Membership and sign-in
Membership determines access. Signing in proves which account is using the service. Installing an app by itself does not establish membership.
Catalog and bookshelf
The catalog is the collection you can search. Your bookshelf or reading list is a smaller set selected for your use. A title in a list is not necessarily downloaded to your device.
Edition and format
An edition is a particular published version. A format is how the content is delivered. Match the assigned title, author, and edition before relying on its page numbering.
Playback position, bookmark, and note
Playback position is where reading currently is. A bookmark marks a place to revisit. A note records your own words. Stopping at a passage does not automatically create a meaningful study note.
Download and synchronization
A download stores content on one device. Synchronization exchanges supported account information while connected. Neither guarantees that a second device has the full book stored for offline use.

Keep a simple reading record: book title, edition, chapter or page, device, and one sentence about where to resume. It gives you a way to recover even when an app does not reopen at the expected position.

1. Establish the right sign-in route

What you will learn: Avoid creating a duplicate account when access is already provided.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Ask whether access is through a school, a household, or an individual adult membership.
  2. For school access, use the school’s instructions. Clever, ClassLink, or Google SSO accounts may require the school sign-in option rather than a manual username-and-password form.
  3. For new individual access, review current membership and eligibility requirements with Learning Ally before registering.

What should happen: You know the correct sign-in method and outstanding membership steps.

If that does not happen: If credentials appear correct but fail, check the sign-in route before repeatedly resetting the password.

Practice: Explain which route you use without disclosing your password.

Source: Learning Ally membership and login help.

2. Choose and open your reading app

What you will learn: Use the official application for your device.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open Learning Ally’s app page and choose the web reader or the download for your supported device.
  2. Sign in with the reader’s approved access method.
  3. Explore the bookshelf and navigation controls before opening an important assignment.

What should happen: You can identify the account and reading environment you are using.

If that does not happen: A school management screen and the student reading screen serve different purposes. Ask the account administrator if you cannot reach the reading area.

Practice: Find the bookshelf, the search function, and the way back to your current book.

Source: Official Learning Ally apps.

3. Search, verify, and add a book to your bookshelf

Start here: The signed-in Learning Ally reading app or browser reader. Have the assigned title, author, and edition information.

Step-by-step instructions

  1. Locate the app’s Search or Browse option through its labeled navigation. Activate Search and enter a distinctive title or author in the search field.
  2. Submit the search once. Read the result titles and inspect likely matches. Compare author and edition with your assignment before adding a book.
  3. Open the intended book’s details and use its add-to-bookshelf action. Confirm the action’s result before returning to the bookshelf.
  4. On the bookshelf, locate the title you added and open it. Identify whether it can be read now or whether the app is downloading content.
  5. If the assigned book is already on the shelf, open that matching edition instead of adding another similarly named title.
  6. Record the selected edition and a chapter title for your first reading task. Keep this record if your class uses printed page references.

Check your result

The correct title is on your bookshelf and you know how to open it again.

If something goes wrong

If the title is missing, confirm the account and clear any bookshelf search/filter. A book added under another account will not necessarily appear here. Ask the instructor about an unavailable edition instead of substituting silently.

Try it yourself

Find an assigned title and explain how its author and edition distinguish it from a similar result.

Documentation: Learning Ally app and bookshelf workflow.

4. Start reading, choose a passage, and mark your place

Start here: An open book in the Learning Ally reader. Keep its assigned passage or chapter written down.

Step-by-step instructions

  1. Explore the player controls with Tab on desktop, or screen-reader focus gestures on mobile. Identify the labeled playback control before activating it. There is no single keyboard shortcut established here for all versions of the app.
  2. Start a short passage and confirm that it is the intended book. Pause playback before investigating navigation so the book narration does not compete with spoken control labels.
  3. Open the available navigation control and inspect its chapter, section, or page options. Choose the passage your edition supports. Read its heading or opening words to confirm where you arrived.
  4. If using a page number, compare the book’s printed-page information with the app’s displayed location. Do not assume an audio position or section count is the printed page number.
  5. Locate the playback-speed control and make one small adjustment. Replay a brief passage and choose the speed at which you can explain its meaning.
  6. Use the player’s bookmark action at a useful study point. If a label or description is offered, make it meaningful, such as “Chapter 2 — review question.”
  7. Move to another passage, open the bookmarks list, and activate the saved item. Verify the returned location against your written record.

Check your result

You can deliberately reach a passage and use a saved marker to return to it.

If something goes wrong

If a control is absent, check that the book is open in the player rather than selected on the bookshelf. Versions differ in available study controls. Use a separate notes document for your explanation if the current reader does not expose a usable note field.

Try it yourself

Read one section, pause, summarize it in your own words, and return to one sentence supporting your summary.

Documentation: Learning Ally reading navigation · Official Android playback and study features.

5. Prepare for reading away from the internet

What you will learn: Check that the book is actually downloaded.

Exactly how to do it

  1. While online in a supported app, add the title to the bookshelf and complete its download.
  2. Open it once before leaving your connection.
  3. At a convenient time, test a short offline reading session. Reconnect afterward.

What should happen: You know whether that device and book are ready for offline use.

If that does not happen: Adding a title is not the same as completing its download. Check the app and download state; do not assume the web reader behaves like an installed app.

Practice: Prepare one book for a trip. Record the device, title, and whether your offline test succeeded.

Source: Offline reading guidance.

6. Troubleshoot without losing your work

What you will learn: Keep useful evidence before changing the setup.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Identify the problem: sign-in, missing title, sound, download, or reading position.
  2. Record the app or browser, device, title, and any error text.
  3. For sign-in problems, confirm the school or individual route. For account questions, use Learning Ally’s Help Center or the school administrator.

What should happen: You have a precise description and the right support route.

If that does not happen: Do not uninstall the app or remove downloads as a first step. First note any reading position or study work you need to keep.

Practice: Describe a hypothetical problem in two sentences: what you tried and what happened instead.

Source: Learning Ally support.

Sign in through the correct school or individual route

Start here: Your approved Learning Ally access information. Ask whether access comes from your school or an individual membership before making a new account.

Step-by-step instructions

  1. Open the official Learning Ally app page and choose the web reader or the appropriate installed app. For the browser reader, use read.learningally.org.
  2. At sign-in, inspect the available routes before typing. If your school uses Clever, ClassLink, or Google single sign-on, follow its school-login instructions. Single sign-on means the school authenticates you through its own approved service.
  3. For a direct account, enter the supplied username and password in their labeled fields, then activate sign-in once. Wait and read the resulting page or error.
  4. Identify the learner’s bookshelf. An educator management account is not necessarily the same view as the learner’s reading app.
  5. For new household or adult access, follow Learning Ally’s membership and eligibility process. Do not assume an app download has completed those steps.
  6. Record the correct route in your private learning notes without recording the password. On a shared device, sign out when finished.

Check your result

You reach the intended reader’s books using the appropriate account route.

If something goes wrong

If school sign-in fails, ask the school to verify access and the approved route. If a direct password is forgotten, use the service’s recovery option. Repeatedly trying another person’s credentials is not a useful test.

Try it yourself

Explain which sign-in route you use and who can help with an account-access problem.

Documentation: Learning Ally account and school-login help · Official reading apps.

Build a study routine with notes and bookmarks

Start here: A familiar passage in a book, with playback paused. Have a separate notes document available as an alternative.

Step-by-step instructions

  1. Record the title, edition, chapter, and page or section. This context makes the note useful even if another device opens at a different location.
  2. Write a short summary or question in your own words. If the installed app provides an accessible note action, open it, enter the note in its labeled field, and activate its save/confirmation control.
  3. If the book and version provide text highlighting, select the intended text using that app’s supported selection method, then apply the available highlight action. Do not assume every audiobook presents selectable full text.
  4. Reopen the saved study item and read it. Confirm it refers to the intended passage rather than relying on an icon or a changed color alone.
  5. If you plan to change devices, reconnect and allow supported study information to synchronize. Check the same note or bookmark on the second device before relying on it.
  6. Keep a short separate record of essential assignment locations. It is useful when the app cannot display a particular study feature or when you need to explain a problem to an instructor.

Check your result

Your study record contains both an idea and enough location information to find its source passage.

If something goes wrong

If a note disappears, check the book edition, account, device connection, and whether you actually saved it. Do not uninstall the app or clear its storage while investigating unsynchronized work.

Try it yourself

Create one summary and one question, then return to their passages without listening from the beginning.

Open and use Learning Ally on iPhone, iPad, or Android

Start here: An approved Learning Ally account and an internet connection. Use the separate VoiceOver or TalkBack directions below.

Step-by-step instructions

  1. From Learning Ally’s official app page, follow the link for your device’s app store. Confirm the application and developer before installing it, then open the app.
  2. Follow the sign-in route your school or membership requires. Pause to identify each field before typing, especially if a school service opens another sign-in screen.
  3. Explore the bookshelf and locate the intended title. Activate it and inspect its reading or download options. Complete a download if you intend to read offline.
  4. Open the book. Navigate to the playback control by its spoken label and activate it. Pause again before navigating chapter or study controls.
  5. Use the app’s chapter, section, or page navigation to select a known passage. Confirm it by its heading or opening words, then resume playback.
  6. Before leaving the app, record your passage and check any bookmark or note you created. If you switch devices, connect both as needed and verify where the book opens.

Check your result

You can use the mobile player through labeled controls and return to a known reading location.

If something goes wrong

If one tap only speaks a control instead of activating it, your mobile screen reader is doing its normal focus action; use its activation gesture below. If the app reports an access error, resolve the membership or login route rather than reinstalling repeatedly.

Try it yourself

Open a book, play a passage, pause it, change chapters, and return using a saved marker.

iPhone and Android: focus, activate, and enter text

iPhone or iPad with VoiceOver

Swipe right with one finger to the next item; swipe left to the previous item. A one-finger double-tap activates the selected item. At a text field, select it and double-tap to edit. With standard typing, select a keyboard key then double-tap; touch typing instead enters the selected key when you lift your finger.

Android with TalkBack

Swipe right or left with one finger to move focus. Double-tap to activate. Use two fingers to scroll. Keyboard entry depends on the keyboard and typing settings; on Gboard, locate a key by touch and lift to enter it.

Use this routine with the reading app

  1. Before activating anything, hear its full label. The book title, a download control, and the Play control perform different actions.
  2. Activate once, wait, and identify the new screen. An app can open an account page or download details rather than immediately playing.
  3. During book narration, pause the player before exploring unfamiliar controls so their labels are easier to hear.
  4. If the expected item is missing, check whether a dialog, keyboard, or menu is still open. Follow the device’s Back or dismissal behavior and identify where you land.

If VoiceOver or TalkBack is off, ordinary touch interaction applies: tap the labeled control to activate it. These touch directions do not define external-keyboard shortcuts. Customized gestures may differ; use your device’s screen-reader help if the standard gesture does something else.

Documentation: Apple VoiceOver operation, VoiceOver typing, TalkBack gestures, and TalkBack typing. Continue with the full VoiceOver manual or TalkBack manual for device-level practice.

Check offline readiness and recover a missing passage

Start here: The installed reading app on the exact device you will use away from the internet. Browser access alone is not proof of offline readiness.

Step-by-step instructions

  1. While connected, select the intended book and complete the app’s download procedure. Check the status until it indicates completion; a title appearing on a shelf is not sufficient.
  2. Open the downloaded book and read a short passage. Note its title, edition, chapter, and page or section.
  3. At a convenient time when disconnecting will not interrupt another task, temporarily disconnect that device and test the same book. Confirm that you can start playback and navigate to a second passage.
  4. Reconnect afterward. If offline playback failed, check storage, download status, and app-specific help while connected. Do not erase other people’s files to free space.
  5. When continuing elsewhere, open the same account and book edition, then compare the current passage with your written location. Use navigation or a bookmark to recover if needed.
  6. Before removing downloads or reinstalling an app, check whether important notes and progress are synchronized or recorded elsewhere.

Check your result

You know whether this book on this device works offline and have a way to regain your place.

If something goes wrong

Report the device, app/version, title, connection state, exact message, and last successful action. Keep passwords and private account information out of support screenshots.

Try it yourself

Demonstrate offline reading for one downloaded title, reconnect, and return to your recorded passage.

Keyboard and control reference

Keyboard access in a desktop reading environment
Purpose Routine
Locate controls Tab forward; Shift+Tab backward; listen to each label
Activate a focused button Enter or Space when supported
Start or pause audio Focus the player’s labeled playback button before activating
Choose a chapter or page Open the player’s navigation control and follow the available choices
Avoid competing voices Pause the book while listening to control labels

A bare Space press is not guaranteed to play or pause in every context. It can affect the page or the currently focused control. On mobile, use your device’s screen-reader gestures instead of desktop keyboard assumptions.

Independent practice

Choose any activity; there is no required order or passing score.

  1. Identify your account’s correct login method.
  2. Find and add the correct edition of a book.
  3. Navigate to an assigned passage and explain its main point.
  4. Use a bookmark to revisit a study question.
  5. Complete an offline test in your installed app before a trip.

After each task, record whether you completed it independently, needed a prompt, or want another attempt. Repeat the difficult step instead of restarting everything.

Topic Missions presents a troubleshooting problem. Command Practice explains and checks one keyboard action at a time. Use Settings to choose speech and sound feedback. Escape returns to the topic list during a session.

When a simulator prompt says protected practice, release the modifier before the final key as instructed. In the real application, use the held keyboard combination in this manual. Completing a simulation does not confirm that the real application received or performed a command.
