Purpose: This manual teaches Google Drive from the beginning to a person using a screen reader. It explains exactly what to press, what should receive focus, what the screen reader should announce, and how to recover when the result is different.

Reference setup: This manual focuses on Google Drive on the web in a current browser, plus practical Drive for desktop and mobile basics.

Screen-reader approach: Google recommends using Drive as a web application rather than as a normal web page. JAWS users generally work with Virtual PC Cursor off; NVDA users generally work in Focus Mode; Narrator users may need Scan Mode off; VoiceOver users should turn Quick Nav off when Drive needs to receive its own keyboard commands.

How to Use This Manual

  • Start with Parts 1-12 before concentrating on your screen reader. Those sections teach Drive itself.

  • Use Alt+N on Windows to open the New menu.

  • Use Slash (/) to begin a Drive search.

  • Use Alt+G, then I to move to the items view.

  • Use Alt+G, then N to move to the navigation panel.

  • Use Ctrl+/ to open Google's current Drive keyboard-shortcuts list.

  • Use Alt+Shift+A for actions that apply to the current folder.

  • Use Spacebar to select or clear selection on an item in the items view.

  • Use Enter to open a selected folder or file.

  • Before sharing, verify both the person and the permission level.

Part 1 - What Google Drive Is

Google Drive is Google's cloud file-storage and collaboration service. It stores files and folders, connects to Google Docs/Sheets/Slides, supports sharing and collaboration, and can synchronize files with a computer.

  • My Drive: files, folders, and shortcuts you created or added to your Drive.

  • Shared with me: files and folders other people shared directly with you.

  • Recent: items you accessed recently.

  • Starred: items you marked for quick access.

  • Trash/Bin: items you deleted but have not yet permanently removed.

  • Shared drives: organization-owned storage used by teams in eligible Google Workspace accounts.

  • Shortcut: a reference to another file or folder; it is not an independent copy.

  • Drive for desktop: Google's Windows/macOS synchronization application.

Part 2 - Open Drive and Identify the Account

2.1 Open Google Drive

What you will learn: Reach your Drive from the browser.

Before you begin: Use the intended Google account.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open Chrome or another supported browser.

  2. Press Ctrl+L.

  3. Type drive.google.com.

  4. Press Enter.

  5. Wait for Drive to load.

  6. Use F6/Tab or the screen reader to locate the Google account/profile control.

  7. Confirm the account name or email address before opening or sharing files.

What should happen: Google Drive opens under the intended account.

If that does not happen: If the wrong account is active, switch Google accounts before creating, uploading, or sharing anything.

Practice: Open Drive and identify the active account.

Part 3 - Learn the Drive Interface

Drive has several major regions. Google documents dedicated keyboard commands to move directly among them.

Task Command
Navigation panel Alt+G, then N
Folders list Alt+G, then F
Items view Alt+G, then I
Page content Alt+G, then C
Details pane Alt+G, then D
Top of application / Google bar Alt+G, then T
Download status Alt+G, then A
Upload status Alt+G, then U

3.1 Move Directly to the Items View

What you will learn: Reach the file/folder list without tabbing through the whole page.

Before you begin: Drive is open.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press Alt+G.

  2. Release the keys.

  3. Press I.

  4. Listen for the items view and the currently focused file/folder.

  5. Use Arrow keys through the items.

What should happen: Focus moves directly to the Drive items area.

If that does not happen: If the shortcut is intercepted, place the screen reader in application/focus mode and try again.

Practice: Move to Items, Navigation, and Details using the Alt+G sequences.

Part 4 - Navigate My Drive and Folders

4.1 Open My Drive

What you will learn: Move from anywhere in Drive to your main Drive storage.

Before you begin: Drive is open.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press Alt+G, then N.

  2. Use Up/Down Arrow through the navigation panel.

  3. Stop on My Drive.

  4. Press Enter.

  5. Press Alt+G, then I to move to its items.

What should happen: My Drive opens and the items view contains its files/folders.

If that does not happen: If My Drive is already selected, pressing Enter may simply refresh focus; use Alt+G then I afterward.

Practice: Open My Drive and identify three items.

4.2 Open and Leave a Folder

What you will learn: Enter a folder and return to its parent.

Before you begin: Focus a folder in the items view.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press Enter.

  2. Wait for the folder to open.

  3. Use Arrow keys through its contents.

  4. To return, move to the Drive breadcrumb/current folder path with Tab/F6 or use browser Back with Alt+Left Arrow when appropriate.

  5. Confirm the parent folder name after returning.

What should happen: The folder opens and you can return to the parent location.

If that does not happen: If Enter previews instead of opens, confirm the focused item is actually a folder.

Practice: Open two folder levels and return.

Part 5 - Select Files and Folders

5.1 Select One Item

What you will learn: Select a file/folder without opening it.

Before you begin: Focus is in the items view.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Use Arrow keys to move to the desired item.

  2. Press Spacebar.

  3. Listen for selected status.

  4. Press Spacebar again to clear selection if needed.

What should happen: The item is selected without opening.

If that does not happen: If Spacebar opens a preview or scrolls, verify the items view has application focus.

Practice: Select and deselect three items.

5.2 Select Multiple Items

What you will learn: Select several items for one action.

Before you begin: Focus the first item.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press Spacebar to select it.

  2. Move with Arrow keys to another item.

  3. Press Spacebar to add it to the selection.

  4. Repeat for a third item.

  5. Use the available actions only after confirming the selected count.

  6. Press Escape or clear selections when finished.

What should happen: Multiple items are selected.

If that does not happen: If moving clears the first selection in your current view, use Ctrl/Shift selection methods available in the shortcuts list or act on one item at a time.

Practice: Select two practice items and clear selection.

Part 6 - Create Folders and Google Files

6.1 Create a New Folder

What you will learn: Create a named folder without using the mouse.

Before you begin: Open the Drive location where the folder should be created.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press Alt+N.

  2. Listen for the New menu.

  3. Use Arrow keys to Folder.

  4. Press Enter.

  5. Type the folder name.

  6. Press Enter.

  7. Wait for Drive's confirmation.

What should happen: A new folder appears in the current location.

If that does not happen: If the New menu does not open, verify the screen reader is in Drive application/focus mode.

Practice: Create a folder named Practice Drive.

6.2 Create a New Google Doc from Drive

What you will learn: Create a cloud document inside the current folder.

Before you begin: Open the destination folder.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press Alt+N.

  2. Move to Google Docs.

  3. Press Enter.

  4. Wait for the new document to open in a new tab.

  5. Rename the document in Google Docs.

  6. Return to Drive with Ctrl+Tab or Ctrl+Shift+Tab as needed.

What should happen: A new Google Doc is created in the folder.

If that does not happen: If the file appears in My Drive instead of the desired folder, verify the folder was open before creating it.

Practice: Create a test Doc and return to Drive.

Part 7 - Upload Files and Folders

7.1 Upload One File

What you will learn: Copy a local computer file into Drive.

Before you begin: Open the Drive folder that should receive the file.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press Alt+N.

  2. Choose File upload.

  3. Wait for the Windows file picker.

  4. Navigate to the file.

  5. Press Enter/Open.

  6. Press Alt+G, then U to move to upload status.

  7. Listen until the upload reports complete.

What should happen: The file uploads to the current Drive folder.

If that does not happen: If the upload fails, check network access, file permissions, storage quota, and organization restrictions.

Practice: Upload a harmless practice file.

7.2 Upload a Folder

What you will learn: Upload a local folder and its contents.

Before you begin: Use a small non-sensitive practice folder.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press Alt+N.

  2. Choose Folder upload.

  3. Select the local folder.

  4. Confirm the upload if the browser asks.

  5. Use Alt+G, then U to monitor status.

  6. Return to Items with Alt+G, then I.

What should happen: The folder and its allowed contents upload to Drive.

If that does not happen: If the browser does not offer Folder upload, use Chrome or upload files individually.

Practice: Upload a small practice folder.

Part 8 - Open and Preview Files

8.1 Open a Google Workspace File

What you will learn: Open Docs, Sheets, or Slides content.

Before you begin: Focus the file.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press Enter.

  2. Wait for the file to open, often in a new browser tab.

  3. Use the relevant Docs/Sheets/Slides accessibility workflow.

  4. Press Ctrl+W when finished if you want to close that tab.

What should happen: The file opens in its editor/viewer.

If that does not happen: If a file opens in Preview instead, activate Open with the relevant Google application.

Practice: Open a test Google Doc and return.

8.2 Preview an Item

What you will learn: Inspect a file without fully opening it.

Before you begin: Focus a supported file.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press Ctrl+Alt+P.

  2. Wait for Preview mode.

  3. Use screen-reader navigation through the preview.

  4. Press Escape to close Preview.

What should happen: The file opens in a Drive preview overlay/view.

If that does not happen: If Preview is not available for that file type, open the file with an appropriate application instead.

Practice: Preview one PDF or image and close it.

Part 9 - Download Files

9.1 Download a File

What you will learn: Save a Drive file to the computer.

Before you begin: Select or focus a downloadable file.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open the item's actions menu with Shift+F10 or the Drive actions controls.

  2. Move to Download.

  3. Press Enter.

  4. Press Ctrl+J in Chrome to review the browser download.

  5. Confirm the filename and status before opening.

What should happen: The file downloads to the browser's configured download location.

If that does not happen: Google-native files may be converted to formats such as DOCX/XLSX/PPTX during download.

Practice: Download one non-sensitive practice file.

Part 10 - Rename, Move, Copy, and Create Shortcuts

10.1 Rename an Item

What you will learn: Change a Drive filename without moving the file.

Before you begin: Select/focus the item.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open the context/actions menu with Shift+F10.

  2. Choose Rename.

  3. Type the new name.

  4. Press Enter.

  5. Listen for confirmation.

What should happen: The item's name changes.

If that does not happen: If the item is shared/organization-owned, your permission may not allow renaming.

Practice: Rename a practice item and rename it back.

10.2 Move an Item

What you will learn: Move a file/folder to another Drive folder.

Before you begin: Select the item.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open the context/actions menu.

  2. Choose Organize or Move.

  3. Navigate the folder picker/tree.

  4. Choose the destination folder.

  5. Activate Move.

  6. Wait for confirmation.

What should happen: The item moves to the selected folder.

If that does not happen: If you do not own the item or lack permission, Move may be unavailable.

Practice: Move a practice file into Practice Drive and back.

10.3 Create a Shortcut Instead of a Copy

What you will learn: Reference an item from another Drive location.

Before you begin: Select the original file/folder.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press Ctrl+C.

  2. Navigate to the folder where you want the shortcut.

  3. Press Ctrl+Shift+V in Chrome.

  4. Listen for Drive to create the shortcut.

What should happen: A shortcut appears in the destination, pointing to the original item.

If that does not happen: The original file's permissions do not automatically change when you create a shortcut.

Practice: Create a shortcut to a practice file.

Part 11 - Star, Recent, Shared with Me, and Activity

11.1 Add or Remove a Star

What you will learn: Mark a frequently used item.

Before you begin: Select an item.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open its context/actions menu.

  2. Choose Organize.

  3. Choose Add to Starred.

  4. Open the Navigation panel with Alt+G, then N.

  5. Move to Starred and press Enter.

  6. Confirm the item appears.

What should happen: The item appears in Starred.

If that does not happen: If the menu says Remove from Starred, it is already starred.

Practice: Star and unstar a practice item.

11.2 Open Recent and Shared with Me

What you will learn: Use Drive's navigation areas rather than searching manually.

Before you begin: Drive is open.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press Alt+G, then N.

  2. Move to Recent and press Enter.

  3. Review items with Alt+G, then I.

  4. Return to Navigation and choose Shared with me.

  5. Review shared items.

What should happen: Recent and Shared with me show their respective item lists.

If that does not happen: If Shared with me is empty, no files may be directly shared to the account.

Practice: Open Recent, Starred, and Shared with me.

Part 12 - Search Google Drive

12.1 Search Drive

What you will learn: Find a file by name or content clue.

Before you begin: Know at least part of the filename, owner, type, or keyword.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press Slash (/).

  2. Type the search term.

  3. Press Enter.

  4. Press Alt+G, then I.

  5. Use Arrow keys through search results.

  6. Press Enter on the desired item.

What should happen: Drive displays matching files/folders.

If that does not happen: If results are too broad, use Drive search filters such as type, owner, date, or location when available.

Practice: Search by filename and by owner.

Find vs Drive search: Slash searches your Drive. Ctrl+F uses the browser/page Find command and is not a substitute for Drive's file search.

Part 13 - Share Files and Folders

13.1 Share with a Specific Person

What you will learn: Give access to an individual account.

Before you begin: Select the file/folder and know the person's exact email address.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open the context/actions menu.

  2. Choose Share.

  3. Move to the People/Email field.

  4. Type the email address.

  5. Select the correct suggestion.

  6. Choose Viewer, Commenter, or Editor when available.

  7. Review the email address and permission.

  8. Add a message if desired.

  9. Activate Send.

What should happen: The person receives access at the selected permission level.

If that does not happen: If the person's account is outside your organization, Drive may show an external-sharing warning. Read it before continuing.

Practice: Open Share on a practice file and inspect the controls without sending.

Permission meaning: Viewer can read. Commenter can comment where supported. Editor can modify content. Folder permissions can affect everything inside the folder.

Part 14 - Permissions and Link Sharing

14.1 Review General/Link Access

What you will learn: Understand whether a file is Restricted or accessible by link.

Before you begin: Open the Share dialog.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Move to General access.

  2. Listen for Restricted, organization-specific access, or Anyone with the link.

  3. Do not change it until you understand who could gain access.

  4. If changing to link access, choose the least powerful role that meets the need.

  5. Copy the link only after verifying access settings.

What should happen: You know who can open the link and what they can do.

If that does not happen: If confidential content is involved, prefer explicit people/group access rather than broad link access unless policy requires otherwise.

Practice: Inspect the General access setting on a practice file.

Critical safety rule: Possessing a link can sometimes be enough to gain access when 'Anyone with the link' is enabled. Treat broadly shared links like credentials.

Part 15 - Version History and File Details

15.1 Open the Details Pane

What you will learn: Review owner, location, activity, and metadata.

Before you begin: Focus a Drive item.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press Alt+G, then D.

  2. Use Tab/arrow keys through the Details pane.

  3. Review file type, owner, location, modified date, and activity when shown.

  4. Press Alt+G, then I to return to the items.

What should happen: The Details pane exposes file information without opening the file.

If that does not happen: If details are unavailable, the current item or permissions may limit what Drive shows.

Practice: Review details for two files.

Version history: Version history behaves differently by file type. Google Docs Editors have detailed edit history inside the editor; uploaded non-Google files may expose Manage versions in Drive when you have permission.

Part 16 - Trash, Restore, and Delete Permanently

16.1 Move an Item to Trash

What you will learn: Remove a file without immediately erasing it permanently.

Before you begin: Select a disposable practice file.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Read the filename again.

  2. Open the context/actions menu.

  3. Choose Move to trash.

  4. Listen for confirmation.

  5. Open Navigation with Alt+G, then N.

  6. Choose Trash/Bin.

  7. Find the item.

What should happen: The item moves to Trash/Bin.

If that does not happen: If the wrong item is selected, cancel before confirming.

Practice: Trash a practice file and locate it.

16.2 Restore an Item

What you will learn: Return a trashed file to Drive.

Before you begin: Open Trash/Bin and select the item.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open the context/actions menu.

  2. Choose Restore.

  3. Listen for confirmation.

  4. Return to My Drive or the original location.

What should happen: The item is restored.

If that does not happen: If Restore is unavailable, you may not own the item or it may have been permanently deleted.

Practice: Restore the practice file.

Permanent deletion: Deleting permanently removes recovery options. Only use Empty trash/Delete forever when you are certain and authorized.

Part 17 - Shared Drives and Organizational Content

Shared drives are available in eligible Google Workspace organizations. Content is generally owned by the organization/team rather than by one individual.

  • Permissions depend on your shared-drive role.

  • Managers have broader control than viewers/commenters/contributors.

  • Moving content into or out of a shared drive can change ownership and access rules.

  • External sharing may be restricted by administrators.

  • Do not assume a file can be deleted, moved, or reshared just because you can open it.

Part 18 - Offline Access and Drive for Desktop

Drive for desktop synchronizes or streams Drive files into Windows File Explorer or macOS Finder. Google notes that after installation you can navigate the Google Drive folder with your normal screen-reader and file-management commands.

18.1 Use Drive for Desktop in Windows File Explorer

What you will learn: Access synchronized Drive files using standard Windows file navigation.

Before you begin: Drive for desktop is installed and signed in.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press Windows+E.

  2. Use the Navigation pane to locate Google Drive.

  3. Press Enter.

  4. Use Arrow keys through files/folders.

  5. Press Enter to open an item.

  6. Use standard File Explorer commands such as F2 Rename, Ctrl+C Copy, Ctrl+V Paste, and Alt+Up Arrow.

What should happen: Drive content behaves like a file-system location, subject to synchronization status and permissions.

If that does not happen: If Google Drive is not present in File Explorer, Drive for desktop may not be installed, signed in, or running.

Practice: Open the Google Drive folder and identify three items.

Sync caution: Moving or deleting synchronized files in File Explorer can also move/delete the cloud copies. Treat local Drive file operations as cloud operations unless you know the item is only cached/downloaded.

Part 19 - JAWS with Google Drive

Google recommends Drive as a web application. JAWS users generally get the best Drive shortcut behavior with the Virtual PC Cursor off.

19.1 Prepare JAWS for Drive

What you will learn: Allow Drive to receive its own application shortcuts.

Before you begin: Drive is open with JAWS.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press JAWS+Z.

  2. Listen for Virtual PC Cursor off.

  3. Press Ctrl+/ to open the Drive shortcut dialog.

  4. Press Escape.

  5. Press Alt+G, then I.

  6. Use Arrow keys through items.

What should happen: Drive shortcuts work and JAWS follows focus.

If that does not happen: If H, K, or other web quick keys take over, the Virtual PC Cursor may be on.

Practice: Toggle into Drive application mode and navigate five items.

19.2 Review the Shortcut Dialog with JAWS

What you will learn: Learn Drive commands from Google itself.

Before you begin: JAWS is running in Drive.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press Ctrl+/.

  2. Move to the shortcut table.

  3. Temporarily turn the Virtual PC Cursor on if needed to use table-reading commands.

  4. Review shortcut names and key combinations.

  5. Turn Virtual PC Cursor off again before returning to Drive application navigation.

  6. Press Escape.

What should happen: You can use the current Google shortcut list rather than relying on outdated memorized commands.

If that does not happen: If the shortcut table is difficult to review, search within the dialog for a command name.

Practice: Look up Move, Share, and Upload.

20.1 Prepare NVDA for Drive

Part 21 - Narrator with Google Drive

21.1 Use Narrator with Drive Application Shortcuts

What you will learn: Turn off Scan Mode when Drive needs direct keyboard control.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press Narrator+Spacebar if necessary to turn Scan Mode off.

Part 22 - VoiceOver with Google Drive

22.1 Prepare VoiceOver for Drive

What you will learn: Let Drive receive its own keyboard shortcuts.

Before you begin: Drive is open on Mac with VoiceOver.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press Left Arrow and Right Arrow together until Quick Nav is off.

  2. Use Option+G, then I for the items view.

  3. Use Option+G, then N for the navigation panel.

  4. Use Arrow keys through Drive items.

  5. Press Spacebar or Return as appropriate to select/open.

What should happen: Drive's application shortcuts work while VoiceOver follows focus.

If that does not happen: If VoiceOver navigation intercepts the keys, check Quick Nav and interaction state.

Practice: Move between Navigation and Items using Option+G sequences.

Part 23 - Braille Displays

Google Drive supports spoken feedback and braille through the active screen reader. Google recommends current browsers, operating systems, and assistive technology.

  • Braille should follow Drive navigation items, file names, folders, dialogs, and sharing controls.

  • Routing keys may activate/select controls depending on the screen reader.

  • Application/focus mode matters for braille just as much as speech.

  • Drive itself does not provide a separate braille translation engine; your screen reader does.

23.1 Test Braille with Drive

What you will learn: Verify navigation, item selection, and dialog access.

Before you begin: Configure the braille display in JAWS, NVDA, Narrator, or VoiceOver.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Move to Navigation panel.

  2. Confirm the selected navigation item appears in braille.

  3. Move to Items.

  4. Read several filenames.

  5. Open the Share dialog on a safe file.

  6. Use routing/navigation keys to move through permission controls.

What should happen: Braille follows Drive focus.

If that does not happen: If braille works in other apps but not Drive, check browser focus and screen-reader application mode first.

Practice: Test Navigation, Items, and Share dialog.

Part 24 - iPhone/iPad and Android Basics

24.1 iPhone/iPad with VoiceOver

  • Swipe right/left to move VoiceOver focus.

  • Double-tap to open the focused file/folder or activate a button.

  • Use the New/Add button to upload or create items.

  • Use More actions for Share, Move, Star, Rename, Make available offline, and other options.

  • Review the sharing permission before sending a link.

24.2 Android with TalkBack

  • Swipe right/left to move TalkBack focus.

  • Double-tap to activate.

  • Google documents double-tap-and-hold on an item to select it, then using the toolbar/More actions for Rename, Move, and related actions.

  • Use a physical keyboard with Drive when you want supported keyboard shortcuts on Android.

Part 25 - Security and Privacy

  • Verify the active Google account before uploading confidential material.

  • Use the least-powerful sharing permission that meets the need.

  • Review 'Anyone with the link' carefully.

  • Do not assume a shared item is safe merely because it appears in Shared with me.

  • Treat suspicious shared files the same way you treat suspicious email attachments.

  • Do not open executable or unexpected downloaded files without verification.

  • Be cautious when granting third-party apps access to Drive.

  • Shared-drive and Workspace rules may be governed by your organization.

25.1 Inspect a Suspicious Shared File Before Opening

What you will learn: Reduce risk from unexpected Drive sharing.

Before you begin: An unfamiliar item appears in Shared with me.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Read the filename and owner.

  2. Open Details rather than opening the file.

  3. Check who shared it and when.

  4. If the sender is unexpected, verify through a trusted channel.

  5. Do not download or open risky file types until verified.

  6. Use Spam/Report controls if Drive marks or allows reporting harmful files.

What should happen: You verify trust before opening the content.

If that does not happen: If you cannot verify the sender or purpose, leave the file unopened.

Practice: Review one harmless shared item using Details.

Part 26 - Troubleshooting

26.1 Drive Shortcuts Do Not Work

What you will learn: Restore the correct application mode.

Before you begin: Drive is open.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press Escape to close menus/dialogs.

  2. JAWS: turn Virtual PC Cursor off.

  3. NVDA: enter Focus Mode.

  4. Narrator: turn Scan Mode off.

  5. VoiceOver: turn Quick Nav off.

  6. Press Alt+G, then I (Option+G then I on Mac).

What should happen: Focus moves to Drive items.

If that does not happen: If not, refresh the page and confirm the browser/keyboard layout is supported.

Practice: Memorize the screen-reader mode check.

26.2 You Cannot Find a File

What you will learn: Use Drive Search instead of browser Find.

Before you begin: Know at least one clue.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press Slash.

  2. Type part of the filename, owner, or keyword.

  3. Press Enter.

  4. Move to Items with Alt+G, then I.

  5. Review results.

  6. Use search filters if needed.

What should happen: The result list narrows to relevant files.

If that does not happen: If you used Ctrl+F, you searched only visible webpage text, not your Drive collection.

Practice: Find one file by name and one by owner.

26.3 You Uploaded a File but Cannot See It

What you will learn: Check upload status and current folder.

Before you begin: A recent upload seems missing.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press Alt+G, then U.

  2. Listen to the upload status.

  3. If complete, press Alt+G, then I.

  4. Confirm the current folder/location.

  5. Use Slash search for the filename.

  6. Check Recent if needed.

What should happen: You determine whether the upload failed, completed elsewhere, or is present but not visible in the current folder.

If that does not happen: If the upload failed, check network/storage/permissions.

Practice: Upload a practice file and verify it three ways.

26.4 You Cannot Move or Rename a Shared Item

What you will learn: Check permission and ownership rather than retrying repeatedly.

Before you begin: The command is missing or disabled.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open Details.

  2. Identify the owner/location.

  3. Open Share and review your role.

  4. Recognize that Viewer/Commenter permissions may not allow organization actions.

  5. Request the needed access from the owner if appropriate.

What should happen: You identify permission as the limiting factor.

If that does not happen: Do not create duplicate copies merely to work around access rules unless allowed and intended.

Practice: Inspect permissions on a shared file.

26.5 You Deleted the Wrong Item

What you will learn: Restore it before permanent deletion.

Before you begin: The item is in Trash/Bin.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open Navigation panel.

  2. Choose Trash/Bin.

  3. Find the item.

  4. Open its actions menu.

  5. Choose Restore.

  6. Return to the original location.

What should happen: The item returns to Drive.

If that does not happen: If it was permanently deleted, recovery may require administrator support and may not be possible.

Practice: Trash and restore a disposable practice file.
