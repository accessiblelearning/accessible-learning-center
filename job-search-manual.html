Accessible Job Search Manual

Find legitimate openings, prepare accessible application documents, complete forms, communicate professionally, and plan accommodations.

Before you begin

  • Create a professional email address and voicemail greeting.
  • Use fictional or redacted information while practicing.
  • Keep passwords, Social Security numbers, banking information, and identity documents out of practice files.
  • Create one Job Search folder with Résumés, Cover Letters, Job Postings, and Applications subfolders.
  • Save a master résumé that is never overwritten; create a separate copy for each application.

Basic how-tos

Choose a job goal and search terms

Keyboard commands: Ctrl+N creates a new Word document. Ctrl+S saves. Ctrl+Shift+S opens Save As in many versions of Word.

  1. Open Word and create a document titled Career Target.
  2. Write one target job title, such as Customer Service Representative.
  3. Add five related titles an employer might use, such as Call Center Representative or Client Support Specialist.
  4. List five skills, three software tools, preferred location, remote or in-person preference, and minimum schedule requirements.
  5. Press Ctrl+S and save the file in your Job Search folder as Career Target.docx.

You should hear: The filename in the Word window title after saving.

If that does not happen: Press Insert+T with JAWS or NVDA+T with NVDA. Confirm the document title and save location before continuing.

Create a clear master résumé

Keyboard commands: Ctrl+Alt+1 applies Heading 1 in Word. Ctrl+B toggles bold. Ctrl+S saves. F7 starts spelling and grammar review.

  1. Create a new Word document and type your name as the first line.
  2. Add professional contact information. Do not include unnecessary personal details such as age, marital status, Social Security number, or a photograph.
  3. Create section headings for Summary, Skills, Work Experience, Education, and Certifications.
  4. Select each section heading and press Ctrl+Alt+1 so it is a real heading, not merely bold text.
  5. List jobs in reverse chronological order. Include employer, job title, dates, and accomplishment-focused bullets.
  6. Begin bullets with action verbs and include measurable results when accurate.
  7. Press F7 and review each suggestion rather than accepting everything automatically.
  8. Press Ctrl+S and save the master as Master Resume.docx.

You should hear: Heading level 1 when moving to each résumé section with H in JAWS or NVDA browse mode after opening a saved or previewed version.

If that does not happen: Use Word’s Styles pane with Ctrl+Alt+Shift+S and apply actual heading styles.

Search a job website efficiently

Keyboard commands: E moves to the next edit field, B to the next button, H by heading, and K by link in JAWS and NVDA browse mode. Ctrl+L focuses the browser address bar.

  1. Open a trusted employer career page or established job-search website.
  2. Confirm the site and employer name from the page title and address.
  3. Press E to find the keyword field. Enter one target title or skill.
  4. Press E again to find the location field and enter a city, state, or Remote.
  5. Use B to find Search and press Enter.
  6. Move through results by headings or links. Open promising postings in a new tab with Ctrl+Enter when supported.
  7. Record the employer, title, location, posting address, and closing date in a search log.

If that does not happen: Use Tab and Shift+Tab to inspect current labels. Do not type until the intended field is announced.

Save and analyze a posting

Keyboard commands: Ctrl+L selects the page address. Ctrl+C copies. Ctrl+N creates a document. Ctrl+V pastes. Ctrl+F finds text on the current page.

  1. Copy the posting address and paste it into a new document.
  2. Record the exact job title, employer, posting number, location, and date found.
  3. Use Ctrl+F on the posting to find headings or words such as qualifications, responsibilities, schedule, salary, and apply.
  4. Create three lists: Required, Preferred, and Questions.
  5. Match each required qualification to honest evidence from your experience.
  6. Save the notes with the employer and job title in the filename.

If that does not happen: Save the posting as accessible text or copy one section at a time. Never rely only on the webpage remaining available.

Complete an online application

Keyboard commands: F or E moves among form fields, B moves among buttons, X among checkboxes, C among combo boxes, and R among radio buttons in JAWS and NVDA browse mode. Insert+F5 in JAWS or NVDA+F7 in NVDA opens an elements list.

  1. Read the entire first page before entering information.
  2. Confirm whether an account is required and save the employer’s official address separately.
  3. Move through fields in order. Listen for the label, required state, instructions, and error message.
  4. Paste information from your résumé only after confirming the field.
  5. For combo boxes, open the list with Alt+Down Arrow, choose with arrow keys, and confirm with Enter.
  6. Upload the targeted résumé and listen for the complete filename.
  7. Review every page before selecting Next.
  8. On the final page, verify contact details, documents, voluntary questions, certifications, and consent statements.
  9. Submit once. Save the confirmation number and a copy of the confirmation page.

If that does not happen: Stop before refreshing. Copy any long answers to a separate document, record the field and error, and try another browser or request an accessible application method.

Advanced how-tos

Tailor a résumé without damaging the master

Keyboard commands: F12 or Ctrl+Shift+S opens Save As in many Word versions. Ctrl+H opens Find and Replace. Shift+F7 opens the thesaurus in supported versions.

  1. Open Master Resume.docx and immediately use Save As.
  2. Name the copy Employer - Job Title - Resume.docx.
  3. Compare the posting’s required skills with your Summary, Skills, and Experience sections.
  4. Move the most relevant truthful qualifications earlier; do not copy claims you cannot support.
  5. Replace vague phrases with specific accomplishments and numbers where accurate.
  6. Run spelling review and use heading navigation to check structure.
  7. Save the Word file, then create a PDF copy only if requested.
  8. Open the PDF with a screen reader and verify reading order, headings, links, and contact information.

If that does not happen: Return to the Word copy. Never submit an inaccessible PDF merely because its visual appearance seems correct.

Write a targeted cover letter

Keyboard commands: Ctrl+N creates a document. Ctrl+S saves. Ctrl+K inserts or edits a link. F7 reviews spelling.

  1. Copy the employer’s name, position title, and two important qualifications into a planning document.
  2. Write a specific opening naming the position and why you are interested.
  3. Use the middle paragraph to connect one or two requirements to concrete experience.
  4. Close by requesting an interview and providing a professional contact method.
  5. Keep the letter concise, accurate, and different from the résumé.
  6. Review organization by paragraph, run F7, and save with the employer and position in the filename.

If that does not happen: Start with three labeled notes—Opening, Evidence, and Closing—then turn each note into a paragraph.

Check whether a posting may be fraudulent

Keyboard commands: Ctrl+L checks the current web address. Ctrl+F searches the page. Alt+Left Arrow returns to the previous page.

  1. Do not use a link from an unexpected text or email until the sender and destination are verified.
  2. Navigate independently to the employer’s official website and locate its Careers page.
  3. Search for the same job title or posting number.
  4. Check whether the recruiter uses an official company email domain.
  5. Treat requests for money, gift cards, cryptocurrency, bank credentials, or equipment purchases as warning signs.
  6. Do not provide identity documents before confirming the employer and legitimate hiring stage.
  7. Save evidence and report suspected fraud to the job site and appropriate authorities.

If that does not happen: Do not continue the application. Contact the employer using a phone number or address obtained independently from its official site.

Request an application or interview accommodation

Keyboard approach: Use a professional email or the employer’s stated accommodation contact. Keep a copy of the request.

  1. Identify the barrier and the part of the process affected.
  2. State that you need a reasonable accommodation to participate in the application or interview process.
  3. Describe the effective change you need, such as an accessible document, keyboard-accessible form, remote interview link, or extra time caused by an inaccessible interface.
  4. Do not volunteer unrelated medical details.
  5. Ask whom to contact and when to expect a response.
  6. Save the request and all replies in the application folder.

If that does not happen: Follow up through the employer’s Human Resources or accommodation channel and document dates, names, and responses.

Prepare for an accessible interview

Keyboard commands: Ctrl+Shift+M mutes or unmutes in Teams; Alt+A in Zoom; Ctrl+D in Google Meet on Windows.

  1. Confirm the interview date, time zone, format, interviewer names, and meeting address.
  2. Research the employer and prepare five accomplishment stories using Situation, Task, Action, and Result.
  3. Test the meeting platform, microphone, headphones, camera choice, screen reader, and internet connection.
  4. Place the job description, résumé, questions, and accommodation plan in easy-to-find files.
  5. Join ten minutes early, muted, and confirm audio.
  6. Afterward, record questions asked, promised follow-up, and a thank-you deadline.

If that does not happen: Use the invitation’s contact method immediately, explain the access problem briefly, and request an alternative connection.

Track applications and follow up

Keyboard commands: In Excel, Ctrl+Arrow moves to the edge of data, Ctrl+Space selects a column, Shift+Space selects a row, and Ctrl+S saves.

  1. Create columns for Employer, Position, Posting address, Date applied, Documents used, Contact, Status, Follow-up date, and Notes.
  2. Enter one application per row.
  3. Use consistent status terms such as Planned, Applied, Interview, Follow-up, Closed, or Offer.
  4. Review the tracker at least twice a week.
  5. Send a concise follow-up when the employer’s timeline allows.
  6. Keep rejection and closed-position records so you can evaluate patterns without reapplying accidentally.

If that does not happen: Use a simple Word table or plain-text list. Consistent information matters more than the program used.

Quick command list

  • Ctrl+L: browser address bar
  • Ctrl+F: find text
  • Ctrl+Enter: open a link in a new tab in common browsers
  • H / K / E / B: next heading, link, edit field, or button in JAWS and NVDA browse mode
  • Insert+F5: JAWS form-fields list
  • NVDA+F7: NVDA elements list
  • Alt+Down Arrow: open many combo boxes
  • Ctrl+S: save
  • F7: spelling and grammar review
  • Ctrl+Alt+1: Heading 1 in Word
