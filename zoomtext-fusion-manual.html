Before you begin

Choose one practice task at a time. Have your usual accessibility tools running and a way to record which steps helped. Use a familiar, non-sensitive document or book for the first exercise.

Keyboard directions

A plus sign means hold the first key while tapping the final key, then release both. For three-key combinations, hold both modifiers while tapping the last key. Do not type the plus sign. “Then” means release the first command before pressing the next key.

Tab moves forward through controls; Shift+Tab moves backward. Enter activates a focused link or button; Space commonly operates buttons and checkboxes. Arrow keys move within menus and lists. Pause after each step to check where focus went. For screen-reader reading and interaction modes, see the Screen Readers manual. Phone gestures are covered in the iPhone and iPad VoiceOver and Android TalkBack manuals.

1. Identify your product and layout

What you will learn: Choose the instructions that match the installed software.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Find the installed product name and version in its Help or About information. Record whether it is ZoomText Magnifier, ZoomText Magnifier/Reader, or Fusion.
  2. For Fusion, confirm whether JAWS is configured for Desktop or Laptop keyboard layout. Keyboard shape alone does not identify the configured layout.
  3. Use the matching command column below. Ask your instructor to check the layout if you are unsure.

What should happen: You know which magnification and speech instructions apply.

If that does not happen: Do not assume a missing reading feature is a fault: ZoomText editions differ, and Fusion uses JAWS for screen reading.

Practice: Explain your product name and which tool supplies speech.

2. Start Fusion and locate its controls

What you will learn: Reach the application without relying on a tiny mouse target.

Exactly how to do it

  1. With Fusion installed, locate its desktop shortcut using the keyboard and press Enter.
  2. Inspect the Fusion interface: Magnifier, Reader, and Tools tabs provide its controls.
  3. On the Reader tab, locate Voice and Rate. Adjust one setting, then return to your practice document.

What should happen: Magnification and speech are available together.

If that does not happen: If Fusion does not start, report the exact launch or licensing message; do not repeatedly start additional copies.

Practice: Locate the magnification and speech controls separately. Change only one and describe the effect.

Source: Getting started with Fusion.

3. Choose useful magnification

What you will learn: Balance readable text with enough surrounding context.

Exactly how to do it

  1. In ZoomText or Fusion Desktop layout, use Caps Lock+Up to increase zoom and Caps Lock+Down to decrease it. Fusion Laptop layout adds Alt to these combinations.
  2. Use the matching 1x toggle in the table below to compare the enlarged view with the whole screen.
  3. Return to your preferred size and read a short paragraph.

What should happen: You can locate both the text and the surrounding controls.

If that does not happen: If a command reads text instead, stop and check the Fusion keyboard layout. Do not keep repeating it.

Practice: Find a heading, move to a paragraph, then find a nearby button. Note which zoom level made all three easiest.

Source: ZoomText magnification.

Source: Fusion magnification layouts.

4. Improve pointer, cursor, and focus visibility

What you will learn: Understand three different visual targets.

Exactly how to do it

  1. In ZoomText, use Caps Lock+P to toggle pointer enhancement, Caps Lock+R for the text cursor, and Caps Lock+F for focus enhancement.
  2. Test the pointer by moving the mouse, the text cursor by editing practice text, and focus by tabbing among buttons.
  3. Toggle each feature again to compare before deciding what helps.

What should happen: You can distinguish the mouse pointer, editing position, and selected control.

If that does not happen: A text-cursor enhancement cannot locate a mouse pointer. First identify which target you are trying to follow.

Practice: Type “My next appointment is Friday.” Find the editing position, then Tab to a control and identify the focus marker.

Source: ZoomText enhancement commands.

5. Set a comfortable listening pace

What you will learn: Use the correct speech controls for your product.

Exactly how to do it

  1. In ZoomText Magnifier/Reader, Caps Lock+Alt+Up or Down changes voice rate. Control briefly quiets speech.
  2. In Fusion, use the Reader tab’s Voice and Rate controls. JAWS supplies speech; ZoomText reading hotkeys are not interchangeable with Fusion speech commands.
  3. Listen to one short passage, adjust one step, and compare comprehension.

What should happen: You can understand the spoken material without repeatedly losing your place.

If that does not happen: Check the product edition, audio output, and voice setting before increasing volume. If another screen reader is also speaking, identify it before changing anything.

Practice: Summarize a short paragraph at two rates. Keep the rate at which you understand and navigate comfortably.

6. Practice a complete work task

What you will learn: Move between overview, detail, and speech deliberately.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open a familiar practice document. Choose a readable magnification level.
  2. Locate its heading, read a paragraph, and find one named word.
  3. Move to another application and back. Identify the current control before typing.
  4. Record the product, keyboard layout, zoom level, and speech preference that worked.

What should happen: Your setup supports a complete task, not only a larger picture.

If that does not happen: When a change makes the task harder, reverse that one change. Avoid changing color, zoom, and voice together because you will not know which helped.

Practice: Repeat with a webpage. Compare whether the same setup works equally well in both applications.

Independent practice

Choose any activity; there is no required order or passing score.

  1. Identify your installed product and configured keyboard layout.
  2. Find a button at your preferred zoom, then compare the whole-screen view.
  3. Explain pointer versus text cursor versus keyboard focus.
  4. Read and summarize a paragraph with a comfortable speech rate.
  5. Troubleshoot an unexpected command by checking product and layout before changing preferences.

After each task, record whether you completed it independently, needed a prompt, or want another attempt. Repeat the difficult step instead of restarting everything.
