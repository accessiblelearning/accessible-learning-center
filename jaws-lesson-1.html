Choose your screen reader

Complete this lesson with JAWS, Windows Narrator, or NVDA. Use one primary screen reader at a time. Follow the column for your selection whenever commands differ.

Goal

Choose JAWS, Narrator, or NVDA and start and stop it safely.

Shared practice steps

  1. Choose one primary screen reader for this course. Do not run multiple screen readers at the same time unless an instructor directs you.
  2. Confirm that the computer’s speakers or headset are connected and the volume is safe.
  3. Start the selected screen reader using its method in the comparison table.
  4. Open Notepad and type one short sentence.
  5. Confirm the screen reader announces the application, focus, and typed text.
  6. Save the note and stop the screen reader using its documented method.

JAWS, Narrator, and NVDA comparison

Commands and differences for Lesson 1
TaskJAWSNarratorNVDA
StartSearch for JAWS from Start; a configured desktop shortcut may also be available.Press Windows+Ctrl+Enter.Press Ctrl+Alt+N if the installation created that shortcut, or search for NVDA from Start.
Screen-reader modifierJAWS key: Insert; Caps Lock may be configured on laptops.Narrator key: Caps Lock or Insert by default.NVDA key: Insert or Caps Lock, based on keyboard layout.
Stop or exitPress Insert+F4 and confirm exit.Press Windows+Ctrl+Enter.Press NVDA+Q and confirm exit.

Command note: JAWS key, Narrator key, and NVDA key each mean that screen reader’s modifier key. Commands can vary by keyboard layout, software version, application, and user settings. Confirm differences in the official references linked from the manual.

Use fictional, public, instructor-provided, or redacted information only. Never submit passwords, license information, account codes, or private workplace data.
