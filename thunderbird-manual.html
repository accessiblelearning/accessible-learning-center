Thunderbird Email Keyboard Manual

Build an independent email routine on Windows, one task at a time.

Before you begin

You need Thunderbird installed, an existing email account, an internet connection for account setup and sending, and access to your provider's sign-in verification method. On a workplace computer, ask your technology team whether this email application is permitted before connecting a work account.

Begin with a personal practice account or a draft with no recipient. Use made-up practice content. Do not use client records in exercises.

Read the keyboard directions

A plus sign means hold the modifier while pressing the final key. For Ctrl+Shift+A, hold Control and Shift, tap A, then release all three. Do not type the plus sign. “Then” means separate actions. Pause after each action and listen before continuing. If your laptop uses function keys for volume or brightness, you may need its Fn key.

Tab moves among controls; Shift+Tab moves backward. Follow the announced control names instead of counting a fixed number of Tab presses. Read this manual in your browser, then switch to Thunderbird before practicing. A command sent to the browser can do something entirely different.

For screen-reader modes and recovery, review the Screen Readers manual and Recovery manual.

1. Connect an existing email account

What you will learn: Connect your account and reach your mailbox without guessing server settings.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open Thunderbird. A new installation offers account setup. Otherwise, open its application menu and choose New Account, then Existing Email. Use Tab to reach the menu, Enter to open it, and arrow keys to explore.
  2. Enter the requested display name, complete email address, and password. Review each field before moving on.
  3. Activate Continue. Wait for the provider configuration results.
  4. Review the account and connection choices. IMAP is normally the proposed option. If automatic setup fails, obtain the exact settings from your provider or technology team.
  5. Activate Done when the settings are correct. If your provider opens a sign-in window, complete its authentication and review the requested access.
  6. Allow time for folders and messages to arrive.

What should happen: Your account and folders become available.

If that does not happen: Read the actual sign-in or configuration error before retrying. Never replace server names with guesses.

Source: Mozilla account configuration

2. Find your place and read a message

  1. Press F6 to move between major areas; Shift+F6 moves backward.
  2. Find the folder pane. Select Inbox with arrow keys.
  3. Move to the message list. Choose a message with Up or Down.
  4. Press Enter to open it. Read with your screen reader's reading commands.
  5. Close the opened message tab with Ctrl+W. Confirm the mailbox is active before continuing.

Practice: Explain aloud whether you are choosing a folder, choosing a message, or reading its contents. If you cannot name the area, pause rather than issuing a deletion or reply command.

Source: Mozilla keyboard shortcuts

3. Write, review, and save a draft

  1. From the mail window, press Ctrl+N for a new message.
  2. For an unsent exercise, leave the recipient empty. To send yourself a practice message, enter your own address in To, then press Enter to confirm that address.
  3. Move to Subject and enter “Email practice.” Move to the message body and write two short sentences.
  4. Review the recipient, subject, and body separately. Check the actual email address, not only its display name.
  5. Press Ctrl+S to save a draft. You can stop here without sending.
  6. Only when you intend to send, activate Send. Then check your account's Sent folder for the copy.

Expected result: You have a saved draft or a sent practice message. These are different outcomes: a draft is unfinished work, not evidence of delivery.

Practice: Write “I practiced reading my message before sending.” Correct one word, then read the complete sentence again.

Source: Mozilla sending and receiving · Draft and compose shortcuts

4. Add a practice attachment

Prepare: Create a small text file named email-practice.txt in a folder you can find. Put only a made-up appointment time inside it.

  1. In your draft, press Ctrl+Shift+A.
  2. In the file picker, locate email-practice.txt and activate Open.
  3. Review the attachment name before deciding whether to send.

Checkpoint: Can you distinguish the document's filename from the email subject? Can you explain what the recipient would receive? Stop if the selected file is not your practice file.

Source: Mozilla attachment shortcut

5. Find a message without losing the rest of your inbox

  1. Select the folder you want to search.
  2. Press Ctrl+Shift+K to reach Quick Filter.
  3. Enter a distinctive subject word or sender name.
  4. Review the available Sender, Recipients, Subject, and Body options. Select the fields relevant to your search.
  5. Check whether Unread, Starred, or Attachment filters are also limiting the results.
  6. Return to the results and inspect the matching messages.
  7. Press Escape as needed to clear the filter and hide the filter bar.

Expected result: Matching messages appear; clearing the filter restores the broader list. Filtering changes which messages you see, not which messages exist.

Practice: Search for “Email practice,” then deliberately use a word that is not in your message. Explain why an empty result does not prove your mail was deleted.

Source: Mozilla Quick Filter

6. Archive a message and locate it again

  1. Select one expendable practice message.
  2. Activate Archive, or press A while the message list has focus.
  3. Find the archive location for that account and locate the message again.

Archiving moves a message out of its current folder. The destination can be an account folder or a local folder, depending on the account's Copies & Folders settings. It remains searchable. Archiving is not a separate backup.

Practice: Before archiving anything important, demonstrate the complete round trip with your practice message: find it, archive it, and find it again.

Source: Mozilla archived messages

7. Troubleshooting practice

The command affected my browser.
Switch to Thunderbird, confirm the active window, and try again.
Typing goes into a search field.
Identify the focused control before repeating the command. Do not assume the message list is active.
My message list suddenly looks empty.
Check the selected folder and clear Quick Filter before assuming mail is missing.
The draft is not in Sent.
Saving and sending are different tasks. Look for the draft and review it before sending.
My screen reader uses the letter command itself.
Check focus and your reader's interaction mode. Use the corresponding Thunderbird menu action if needed. Do not turn off speech and continue blindly.
I cannot explain an error.
Record the exact error text, the action immediately before it, and whether it affects one account or all accounts. Share that information with your instructor or technology team.

8. Independent practice tasks

Choose any task. There is no required order or passing score. Use the instructions again whenever you need them.

  1. Orientation: Find Inbox and identify one message's sender and subject. Describe your current location.
  2. Draft: Write a three-sentence introduction without a recipient. Save it and explain why it has not been sent.
  3. Attachment: Add email-practice.txt and verify the filename.
  4. Search: Find your practice subject, then restore the unfiltered list.
  5. Recovery: Ask an instructor to select a different folder. Identify what changed before searching.
  6. Organization: Archive a practice message and retrieve it.

After each task, note what you completed independently, what prompt helped, and what you want to repeat. These are practice activities in Thunderbird; this manual does not yet add an automated Thunderbird Mission Control lesson.
