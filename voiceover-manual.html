Independent Self-Study Guide

Purpose: This manual teaches iPhone and iPad from the beginning to a person using VoiceOver. It explains where controls are physically located, what gesture to use, what VoiceOver should announce, how to recover when the result is different, and how to practice each skill independently.

Reference version: The manual is based on Apple's current iOS 26 and iPadOS 26 VoiceOver guidance. Some screens and feature names can vary by device model, carrier, region, and installed apps.

Device differences: iPhone models with Face ID use the Side button and edge gestures. Older iPhone models and some iPads have a physical Home button. iPads may use a top button with Touch ID or Face ID depending on model.

How to Use This Manual

  • Practice new gestures first in Settings > Accessibility > VoiceOver > VoiceOver Practice so a mistake does not activate something unexpectedly.

  • VoiceOver changes ordinary touch gestures. A single tap selects and speaks an item; a double-tap activates the selected item.

  • Swipe right moves to the next item. Swipe left moves to the previous item.

  • A two-finger scrub means move two fingers back and forth quickly three times in a Z shape. It commonly means Back, Cancel, or dismiss.

  • A two-finger double-tap is the Magic Tap. Its action depends on context, such as answering/hanging up a call or starting/stopping media or dictation.

  • Three-finger swipes scroll pages and lists. Four-finger gestures move quickly to screen edges or among apps.

  • The rotor changes what an up/down swipe does. Learn the rotor early; it is central to efficient VoiceOver use.

  • If you become disoriented, touch the screen to hear what is under your finger, then swipe left/right to rebuild context.

  • For passwords or other sensitive information, use headphones or silent braille input when privacy matters.

Part 1 - Physical Orientation: iPhone and iPad

1.1 iPhone with Face ID

  • Hold the iPhone with the screen facing you and the charging/data port at the bottom.

  • The Side button is on the right edge. It wakes/locks the phone and is used for the Accessibility Shortcut when configured.

  • Volume Up and Volume Down are on the left edge. The Action button or Ring/Silent control is also on the left edge, depending on model.

  • The front-facing camera/sensor area is near the top of the screen. The speaker/earpiece is near the top edge.

  • The charging port is centered on the bottom edge on current models; speakers/microphones are also along the bottom edge.

1.2 iPhone with Home Button

  • The Home button is centered below the screen. Press it to go Home; double-click it for the App Switcher.

  • The Side or top sleep/wake button is used to lock/wake the phone, depending on model.

  • Volume controls are on the left edge.

1.3 iPad

  • Hold the iPad with the screen facing you. The charging port is centered on one short edge; exact speaker positions depend on model.

  • The top button is on an outer edge and wakes/locks the iPad. On some models it also contains Touch ID.

  • Volume controls are near the top button on current models, but position varies by iPad generation.

  • Some iPads have a physical Home button centered below the screen; newer models do not.

  • Because hardware layouts vary, identify your specific model in Settings > General > About before memorizing physical positions.

Part 2 - Turn VoiceOver On and Off

2.1 Turn VoiceOver On in Settings

What you will learn: Enable the built-in screen reader.

Before you begin: You can currently see or have sighted assistance for the initial setup.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open Settings.

  2. Choose Accessibility.

  3. Choose VoiceOver.

  4. Turn VoiceOver on.

  5. After VoiceOver starts, remember that one tap selects and a double-tap activates.

What should happen: VoiceOver begins speaking the selected control.

If that does not happen: If VoiceOver does not speak, raise media volume and verify VoiceOver is actually on.

Practice: Turn it on and navigate back one screen.

2.2 Use Siri

What you will learn: Turn VoiceOver on/off without navigating Settings.

Before you begin: Siri is enabled.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Activate Siri with the Side/top/Home button or by voice if configured.

  2. Say, Turn on VoiceOver.

  3. To turn it off later, ask Siri, Turn off VoiceOver.

What should happen: Siri changes the VoiceOver state.

If that does not happen: If Siri cannot change the setting, use Settings or the Accessibility Shortcut.

Practice: Practice asking Siri for VoiceOver status.

2.3 Configure the Accessibility Shortcut

What you will learn: Toggle VoiceOver with a hardware-button triple-click.

Before you begin: VoiceOver is already configured enough to navigate Settings.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open Settings > Accessibility.

  2. Move to Accessibility Shortcut near the bottom.

  3. Activate it.

  4. Select VoiceOver.

  5. On iPhone with Face ID, triple-click the Side button. On devices with a Home button, triple-click the Home button. On iPad without Home button, triple-click the top button.

What should happen: VoiceOver toggles on/off or the shortcut menu appears if more than one accessibility feature is selected.

If that does not happen: If nothing happens, verify VoiceOver is checked in Accessibility Shortcut and click quickly three times.

Practice: Toggle VoiceOver with the shortcut once.

Part 3 - VoiceOver Practice and Core Gestures

3.1 Open VoiceOver Practice

What you will learn: Learn gestures without triggering real actions.

Before you begin: VoiceOver is on.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open Settings > Accessibility > VoiceOver.

  2. Find VoiceOver Practice.

  3. Double-tap to open it.

  4. Try one-, two-, three-, and four-finger taps, double taps, triple taps, and swipes.

  5. Listen to VoiceOver describe each gesture.

  6. Activate Done and double-tap to exit.

What should happen: Gestures are announced without performing their normal action.

If that does not happen: If a gesture performs a real action, you may not be in VoiceOver Practice. Return to Settings and reopen it.

Practice: Spend at least five minutes practicing flicks and multifinger gestures.

Part 4 - Select, Activate, and Explore by Touch

4.1 Explore by Touch

What you will learn: Find controls spatially without activating them.

Before you begin: Open any familiar screen.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Place one finger lightly on the screen.

  2. Slide the finger slowly without lifting.

  3. Listen as VoiceOver announces each item under your finger.

  4. Stop when you hear the item you want.

  5. Lift your finger.

  6. Double-tap anywhere on the screen to activate the selected item.

What should happen: The selected item activates even if you double-tap away from its visual position.

If that does not happen: If the wrong item activates, you changed selection before double-tapping. Re-select the intended item and try again.

Practice: Find three Home Screen apps by touch and open one.

4.2 Use Sequential Navigation

What you will learn: Move predictably through every item.

Before you begin: Any screen is open.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Swipe right for the next item.

  2. Swipe right repeatedly and listen to the order.

  3. Swipe left to move backward.

  4. Double-tap the selected item when ready.

What should happen: VoiceOver moves one accessible item at a time.

If that does not happen: If swipes scroll instead of moving item-by-item, make the one-finger flick quicker and shorter.

Practice: Move through ten items forward and five backward.

Part 5 - Scroll, Go Home, and Switch Apps

5.1 Scroll a Page or List

What you will learn: Move through content larger than one screen.

Before you begin: A scrollable list/page is open.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Place three fingers on the screen with space between them.

  2. Swipe up to scroll down to later content, or swipe down to scroll toward earlier content.

  3. Listen for the page/row announcement and continue with one-finger navigation.

What should happen: The screen scrolls by roughly one page/region.

If that does not happen: If nothing moves, the current area may not scroll or the gesture may have been interpreted as separate touches.

Practice: Scroll through Settings and back.

5.2 Go Home on Face ID Devices

What you will learn: Return to the Home Screen with VoiceOver.

Before you begin: Use an iPhone/iPad without a Home button.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Place one finger at the bottom edge.

  2. Drag upward slowly.

  3. Listen/feel for the first tone/vibration, then continue until you hear two rising tones.

  4. Lift your finger at two tones.

What should happen: The Home Screen opens.

If that does not happen: If the App Switcher opens, you dragged too far; use the two-finger scrub and try again, lifting at two tones.

Practice: Go Home from three apps.

5.3 Open the App Switcher

What you will learn: Move among recently used apps.

Before you begin: Use a Face ID/no-Home-button device.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Place one finger at the bottom edge.

  2. Drag upward.

  3. Continue past the Home cue until you hear three tones / the second stronger vibration.

  4. Lift your finger.

  5. Swipe left/right through app cards.

  6. Double-tap the desired app.

What should happen: The selected recent app opens.

If that does not happen: If Home opens, you lifted too early. Try again and continue to the three-tone cue.

Practice: Switch between two apps three times.

Home-button devices: Press Home to go Home. Double-click Home to open the App Switcher.

Part 6 - Lock Screen and Unlocking

6.1 Unlock with Face ID

What you will learn: Unlock without sighted assistance.

Before you begin: Face ID is set up.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Wake the device with the Side/top button or by raising/tapping if configured.

  2. Face the device so the camera can see you.

  3. After authentication, drag one finger up from the bottom edge until you hear two rising tones, then lift.

  4. If asked for a passcode, enter it carefully.

What should happen: The Home Screen or previous app opens.

If that does not happen: If Face ID fails, reposition the device and try again; eventually the passcode may be required.

Practice: Lock and unlock the device twice.

Passcode privacy: Apple notes that handwriting or onscreen braille can be used to enter a passcode without having digits spoken aloud. Headphones are another practical privacy option.

Part 7 - Status Bar, Control Center, and Notifications

7.1 Read the Status Bar

What you will learn: Check time, battery, Wi-Fi, and other indicators.

Before you begin: Any normal screen is visible.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Touch near the top status area until VoiceOver announces a status item.

  2. Swipe left/right to hear time, battery, Wi-Fi/cellular strength, and other status information.

What should happen: VoiceOver reads status indicators.

If that does not happen: If you select an app control instead, move closer to the top edge or use four-finger navigation to the first item.

Practice: Check battery and network status.

7.2 Open Control Center

What you will learn: Reach quick controls.

Before you begin: VoiceOver is on.

Exactly how to do it

  1. On Face ID/no-Home-button devices, drag one finger down from the top edge until you hear two rising tones, then lift.

  2. Alternative on all models: select an item in the status bar, then swipe up with three fingers.

  3. Swipe left/right through Control Center controls.

  4. Use double-tap to activate a button or double-tap-and-hold when a control supports additional options.

  5. Use a two-finger scrub to dismiss Control Center.

What should happen: Control Center opens and closes.

If that does not happen: If Notification Center opens, your edge drag went too far; try again and lift at the second tone instead of the third.

Practice: Open Control Center, check Wi-Fi, and dismiss it.

7.3 Open Notifications

What you will learn: Review alerts without using the visual screen.

Before you begin: VoiceOver is on.

Exactly how to do it

  1. On Face ID/no-Home-button devices, drag one finger down from the top edge until you hear three tones, then lift.

  2. Alternative: select the status bar and swipe down with three fingers.

  3. Swipe right/left through notifications.

  4. Use the Actions rotor when available for Clear, Options, or other actions.

  5. Use a two-finger scrub to dismiss.

What should happen: Notification Center appears and can be dismissed.

If that does not happen: If Control Center opens instead, continue the edge gesture farther until the third tone.

Practice: Open notifications and read two alerts.

Part 8 - Home Screen, App Library, and Search

8.1 Open an App

What you will learn: Launch an app from the Home Screen.

Before you begin: You are on the Home Screen.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Swipe left/right until the app name is selected, or explore by touch.

  2. Double-tap.

What should happen: The app opens.

If that does not happen: If a context menu opens, you may have double-tapped and held too long; two-finger scrub to dismiss and try a quicker double-tap.

Practice: Open Settings, return Home, then open Safari.

8.2 Search from the Home Screen

What you will learn: Find an app quickly.

Before you begin: You are on the Home Screen outside the status bar.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Tap a Home Screen item.

  2. Swipe down with three fingers.

  3. Type the app or item name in Search.

  4. Swipe through results.

  5. Double-tap the desired result.

What should happen: The selected search result opens.

If that does not happen: If the Home Screen scrolls instead, make sure VoiceOver focus is on the Home Screen first.

Practice: Search for Settings and Files.

8.3 Use Actions on an App

What you will learn: Open app-specific Home Screen actions without dragging icons.

Before you begin: An app icon is selected.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Rotate the rotor to Actions, or swipe up/down if Actions is already available.

  2. Swipe up/down through actions such as Edit Mode, Remove App, or app-specific shortcuts.

  3. Double-tap the desired action.

What should happen: The selected action runs.

If that does not happen: If Actions is not available, the selected item may not support actions or the rotor is on another setting.

Practice: Inspect Actions for two apps without deleting anything.

Part 9 - The VoiceOver Rotor

9.1 Use the Rotor

What you will learn: Change what one-finger up/down swipes do.

Before you begin: VoiceOver is on.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Place two fingers on the screen as if touching a small dial.

  2. Rotate them clockwise or counterclockwise.

  3. Listen as VoiceOver announces rotor settings.

  4. Stop on a setting such as Characters, Words, Headings, Links, Actions, Speaking Rate, or Volume.

  5. Swipe up/down with one finger to use the selected rotor setting.

What should happen: Up/down swipes now perform the selected rotor function.

If that does not happen: If the rotor does not turn reliably, use one finger from each hand: move one upward while the other moves downward at the same time.

Practice: Select Words and move through a paragraph, then choose Characters.

9.2 Customize the Rotor

What you will learn: Keep only useful rotor items.

Before you begin: You can navigate Settings.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open Settings > Accessibility > VoiceOver > Rotor.

  2. Swipe through available rotor items.

  3. Select the ones you want available.

  4. Reorder them if desired.

  5. Return to an app and test the rotor.

What should happen: The rotor contains your preferred settings in a more efficient order.

If that does not happen: If an item is missing in an app, rotor options also depend on the current context.

Practice: Keep at least Characters, Words, Actions, and Headings/Links for web use.

Part 10 - Onscreen Keyboard and Typing Modes

10.1 Standard Typing

What you will learn: Enter text by selecting each key and double-tapping.

Before you begin: Focus an editable text field and double-tap it.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Swipe left/right through keyboard keys or explore the keyboard by touch.

  2. When the correct key is selected, double-tap anywhere.

  3. Continue until the word is entered.

What should happen: Characters are entered one at a time.

If that does not happen: If a key is only announced but not entered, remember Standard typing requires activation with a double-tap.

Practice: Type your first and last name.

10.2 Touch Typing

What you will learn: Type faster by lifting your finger on the desired key.

Before you begin: VoiceOver typing style is set to Touch Typing.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Place a finger on the keyboard.

  2. Slide until VoiceOver announces the desired key.

  3. Lift your finger to enter it.

  4. Repeat for each character.

What should happen: The character enters when your finger lifts.

If that does not happen: If keys require double-tap, you are still using Standard typing. Change Typing Style in VoiceOver settings or via Typing Mode rotor.

Practice: Type one sentence.

10.3 Dictation from the Keyboard

What you will learn: Enter text by voice.

Before you begin: The onscreen keyboard is visible and Dictation is enabled.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Double-tap with two fingers on the keyboard.

  2. Speak clearly.

  3. Double-tap with two fingers again to stop dictation.

  4. Review and correct the text before sending or saving.

What should happen: Spoken words appear as text.

If that does not happen: If media starts/stops instead, focus may not be in the keyboard. Select an edit field and try again.

Practice: Dictate one sentence and correct one word manually.

Part 11 - Edit, Select, Copy, and Paste Text

11.1 Move the Insertion Point

What you will learn: Edit text by character, word, or line.

Before you begin: An editable field contains text.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Rotate the rotor to Characters, Words, or Lines.

  2. Swipe up/down to move the insertion point backward/forward according to the rotor unit.

  3. Listen to the item VoiceOver announces at each movement.

What should happen: The insertion point moves through the text predictably.

If that does not happen: If the cursor does not move, the text may not be editable or focus may be outside the field.

Practice: Move to the third word of a sentence.

11.2 Select, Copy, and Paste

What you will learn: Use the rotor Edit actions.

Before you begin: The insertion point is in editable text.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Rotate the rotor to Edit.

  2. Swipe up/down until Select or Select All is announced.

  3. Double-tap.

  4. Return to Edit rotor and choose Copy.

  5. Move to the destination edit field.

  6. Choose Paste from the Edit rotor and double-tap.

What should happen: Selected text is copied and pasted.

If that does not happen: If Edit is not in the rotor, add it in Settings > Accessibility > VoiceOver > Rotor.

Practice: Copy one phrase into Notes.

Part 12 - Dictation, Siri, and Magic Tap

12.1 Use Siri for Common Tasks

What you will learn: Complete tasks by voice while VoiceOver is running.

Before you begin: Siri is enabled.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Activate Siri.

  2. Say a simple request such as Open Messages, Call a contact, Set a timer for five minutes, or Turn on VoiceOver.

  3. Listen to Siri's response and use VoiceOver to inspect any result shown onscreen.

What should happen: Siri performs or offers the requested action.

If that does not happen: If Siri mishears a name or command, repeat more specifically or use touch navigation.

Practice: Use Siri to open two apps and set a timer.

Magic Tap: Two-finger double-tap is context-sensitive. It can answer/end a call, pause/resume media, or start/stop some activities. Always listen to the current context before using it.

Part 13 - Safari and Web Navigation

13.1 Open a Website

What you will learn: Enter a web address and load a page.

Before you begin: Safari is open.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Move to the address/search field.

  2. Double-tap to edit it.

  3. Type or dictate the address.

  4. Activate Go on the keyboard.

What should happen: Safari loads the website.

If that does not happen: If search suggestions appear, continue typing the full address or choose the intended suggestion.

Practice: Open apple.com/accessibility.

13.2 Navigate by Headings and Links

What you will learn: Move through a webpage efficiently.

Before you begin: A structured webpage is loaded.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Rotate the rotor to Headings.

  2. Swipe down to move to the next heading; swipe up for the previous heading.

  3. Rotate the rotor to Links.

  4. Swipe up/down through links.

  5. Double-tap the selected link to open it.

What should happen: VoiceOver jumps by webpage structure instead of reading every item.

If that does not happen: If Headings or Links are missing, customize the rotor and make sure focus is in web content.

Practice: Find three headings and two links on an Apple Support page.

Web forms: When a text field is selected, double-tap to begin editing, enter text, then use the rotor/Next controls to move among form fields. Review all information before submitting.

Part 14 - Messages

14.1 Read a Conversation

What you will learn: Open and review messages.

Before you begin: Messages is open.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Swipe through the conversation list.

  2. Double-tap the desired conversation.

  3. Swipe through message bubbles and timestamps.

  4. Use the Actions rotor when available for reply, reaction, copy, or other contextual actions.

What should happen: VoiceOver reads the conversation in order.

If that does not happen: If VoiceOver jumps to the compose field, swipe backward to earlier messages.

Practice: Read the last five messages in a safe conversation.

14.2 Send a Message

What you will learn: Compose and verify a text before sending.

Before you begin: A conversation is open.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Move to the message text field and double-tap.

  2. Type or dictate the message.

  3. Use the rotor to review by words/characters.

  4. Move to Send.

  5. Double-tap only after confirming recipient and content.

What should happen: The message sends to the selected conversation.

If that does not happen: If you are unsure of the recipient, use the Back button and verify the conversation title before sending.

Practice: Draft a harmless message and review it before sending or clearing.

Part 15 - Phone and FaceTime on iPhone

15.1 Answer and End a Call

What you will learn: Use VoiceOver during an incoming call.

Before you begin: An incoming call is ringing.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Use the two-finger double-tap (Magic Tap) to answer when appropriate.

  2. During the call, use VoiceOver to locate Mute, Speaker, Keypad, Audio, or other controls.

  3. Use the Magic Tap again to end the call when supported, or select End and double-tap.

What should happen: The call connects and later disconnects.

If that does not happen: If Magic Tap controls media instead, the call interface may not have focus; use the onscreen Answer/End control.

Practice: Practice locating call controls with a trusted caller.

FaceTime: FaceTime uses similar VoiceOver navigation for microphone, camera, speaker, effects, participants, and End. Verify camera state before joining or answering video calls.

Part 16 - Mail

16.1 Read Email

What you will learn: Open a message and navigate its content.

Before you begin: Mail is configured.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open Mail.

  2. Swipe through the message list.

  3. Double-tap a message.

  4. Use normal swipe navigation or rotor settings such as Headings/Links when available in rich email content.

  5. Use Actions for reply, flag, move, delete, or other message actions.

What should happen: The email opens and can be reviewed.

If that does not happen: If an email appears suspicious, do not activate links or attachments until the sender/domain is verified.

Practice: Read one safe message and locate Reply without activating it.

16.2 Compose Email

What you will learn: Send an email carefully.

Before you begin: Mail is open.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Activate Compose.

  2. Enter the recipient and verify the suggested address.

  3. Enter Subject.

  4. Enter the message body.

  5. Review recipient, subject, and body.

  6. Activate Send.

What should happen: The message sends to the intended address.

If that does not happen: If autocomplete shows similar contacts, type more of the address and verify the exact recipient before selecting.

Practice: Compose a draft and save it without sending.

Part 17 - Settings and VoiceOver Customization

  • Speaking Rate: Settings > Accessibility > VoiceOver. Adjust the slider or add Speaking Rate to the rotor.

  • Speech/Voice: choose preferred language, voice, and pronunciation options.

  • Verbosity: control punctuation, hints, table/list announcements, text deletion feedback, and more.

  • Audio: configure audio ducking, sounds/haptics, and routing options.

  • Commands: Settings > Accessibility > VoiceOver > Commands lets you customize touch gestures, keyboard shortcuts, Magic Tap, handwriting, and braille commands.

  • Typing: choose Standard, Touch, or Direct Touch typing and adjust typing feedback.

17.1 Customize a Gesture Safely

What you will learn: Assign a VoiceOver command to a gesture.

Before you begin: You understand the default gesture you are changing.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open Settings > Accessibility > VoiceOver > Commands.

  2. Choose Touch Gestures.

  3. Find the gesture.

  4. Choose the new VoiceOver command.

  5. Test it in VoiceOver Practice.

  6. Use Reset VoiceOver Commands if customization causes confusion.

What should happen: The gesture performs the assigned command.

If that does not happen: Do not remap core navigation gestures until you are confident you can recover.

Practice: Inspect custom gesture options without changing Swipe Left/Right.

Part 18 - Screen Curtain and Privacy

18.1 Turn Screen Curtain On and Off

What you will learn: Keep the display visually dark while VoiceOver remains usable.

Before you begin: VoiceOver is on.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Triple-tap the screen with three fingers.

  2. Listen for Screen Curtain on.

  3. Use the device normally with VoiceOver.

  4. Triple-tap with three fingers again to turn it off.

What should happen: The display becomes visually dark while VoiceOver continues speaking.

If that does not happen: If speech is muted instead, your gesture may have been interpreted differently; retry with three clearly separated fingers.

Practice: Turn Screen Curtain on, navigate one screen, then turn it off.

Privacy limit: Screen Curtain protects against casual visual observation, but it does not secure data from apps, recordings, screen sharing, or account access.

Part 19 - VoiceOver Recognition and Image Descriptions

19.1 Enable VoiceOver Recognition

What you will learn: Get generated descriptions when apps/web content lack accessibility labels.

Before you begin: Use a supported iPhone/iPad model.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open Settings > Accessibility > VoiceOver.

  2. Open VoiceOver Recognition.

  3. Review Image Descriptions and other available recognition options.

  4. Enable only the features you want.

  5. Test on a non-sensitive image.

What should happen: VoiceOver may provide generated descriptions for unlabeled images/text/interfaces.

If that does not happen: Generated descriptions can be incomplete or wrong; do not use them as the sole basis for safety-critical decisions.

Practice: Compare an author-provided alt text image with an automatically described image.

Part 20 - Braille Screen Input

Braille Screen Input lets you type braille directly on the touchscreen without a physical braille display. Exact gestures and layouts can be customized in VoiceOver settings.

20.1 Add Braille Screen Input to the Rotor

What you will learn: Make touchscreen braille available quickly.

Before you begin: VoiceOver is on.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open Settings > Accessibility > VoiceOver > Rotor.

  2. Enable Braille Screen Input.

  3. Return to an editable text field.

  4. Rotate the rotor until Braille Screen Input is announced.

  5. Follow the orientation prompts and place fingers as instructed.

What should happen: The touchscreen becomes a braille-entry surface.

If that does not happen: If the dots feel reversed or misplaced, review Braille Screen Input orientation/calibration options before typing sensitive information.

Practice: Enter a short practice word.

Passcode use: Braille Screen Input can be useful for entering sensitive text without having characters spoken aloud, but learn the layout thoroughly before relying on it for a passcode.

Part 21 - Refreshable Braille Displays

21.1 Pair a Braille Display

What you will learn: Connect a supported refreshable braille device to VoiceOver.

Before you begin: The braille display is powered on and in its Bluetooth/Apple pairing mode.

Exactly how to do it

  1. On iPhone/iPad, open Settings > Accessibility > VoiceOver > Braille.

  2. Wait for the display to appear in the list.

  3. Double-tap the display name.

  4. Enter a pairing code if the display requires one.

  5. Wait for VoiceOver and braille output to confirm connection.

What should happen: The braille display shows VoiceOver content.

If that does not happen: If the display is not found, verify Bluetooth/pairing mode, remove stale pairings, and consult the display manufacturer's Apple-specific instructions.

Practice: After pairing, test output, input, and routing.

Connection rule: Pair the display first, verify it is active in VoiceOver, then test three separate functions: OUTPUT (read text), INPUT (type text), and ROUTING (move the cursor/focus with routing keys).

  • VoiceOver supports device-specific braille commands in addition to common braille commands.

  • Braille panning moves through content wider than the display.

  • Routing keys generally move VoiceOver focus or the text insertion point to the corresponding braille cell when supported.

  • Braille tables, contracted/uncontracted braille, status cells, and input/output settings are configured under VoiceOver > Braille.

Part 22 - Braille Access

In iOS/iPadOS 26, Apple provides Braille Access for supported braille-display workflows. It can turn the device into a more braille-centered environment for apps, calculations, BRF files, transcription, and other tasks.

  • Apple documents dot 4 + Space for Next and dot 1 + Space for Previous in Braille Access.

  • Dots 1-2-3 + Space moves to the first menu item; dots 4-5-6 + Space moves to the last.

  • Dots 1-2 + Space performs Back; dot 8 selects/returns; dots 1-2-4 + Space searches text.

  • Dots 7+8 exits Braille Access in Apple's current guide.

Caution: Braille Access is a distinct mode. Learn how to exit before using it independently.

Part 23 - External Keyboards

VoiceOver can be controlled with an Apple or Bluetooth external keyboard. Keyboard commands vary by the VoiceOver modifier and layout settings.

  • Configure an external keyboard in Settings > Bluetooth or the hardware keyboard settings.

  • Use VoiceOver Help/keyboard help to learn commands rather than guessing.

  • If a keyboard shortcut is intercepted by an app, use VoiceOver pass-through/ignore-next-key features as documented for the current platform.

  • External keyboards can be especially useful on iPad for long-form typing and web/application navigation.

Part 24 - Files, Downloads, and Sharing

24.1 Find a Download in Files

What you will learn: Locate a file downloaded from Safari or another app.

Before you begin: A download has completed.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open Files.

  2. Navigate to Browse.

  3. Open Downloads or the location used by your browser/account.

  4. Swipe through filenames.

  5. Double-tap the desired file.

What should happen: The file opens in a compatible app/preview.

If that does not happen: If it is not in Downloads, check iCloud Drive, On My iPhone/iPad, or the app-specific folder.

Practice: Download a safe small file and locate it.

24.2 Use the Share Sheet

What you will learn: Send or save content through another app.

Before you begin: A file, webpage, photo, or other shareable item is selected.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Activate Share.

  2. Swipe through suggested people/apps/actions.

  3. Verify the destination carefully.

  4. Double-tap the intended action such as Save to Files, Mail, Messages, Copy, or AirDrop.

  5. Complete the destination-specific dialog.

What should happen: The item is shared or saved through the selected destination.

If that does not happen: If a sensitive item is selected, cancel with the two-finger scrub rather than guessing.

Practice: Open the Share Sheet on a harmless webpage and cancel it.

Part 25 - App-Specific Actions and the Actions Rotor

25.1 Use Actions

What you will learn: Perform contextual commands without hunting for buttons.

Before you begin: Select an item that supports actions, such as a Mail message or notification.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Rotate the rotor to Actions if needed.

  2. Swipe up/down through available actions.

  3. Listen to each action before activating it.

  4. Double-tap the desired action.

What should happen: The contextual action runs.

If that does not happen: Actions depend on the selected item; if none are available, move to a different item or rotor setting.

Practice: Inspect Actions for a Mail message and a Home Screen app.

Part 26 - iPad Multitasking and Dock

26.1 Open the Dock with VoiceOver

What you will learn: Reach recently used/favorite apps without going Home.

Before you begin: Use an iPad without a Home button or with gesture navigation.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Place one finger at the bottom edge.

  2. Drag upward until you hear two rising tones.

  3. Swipe down after the two-tone cue as Apple documents for the Dock.

  4. Swipe through Dock apps.

  5. Double-tap an app.

What should happen: The Dock appears and the app opens.

If that does not happen: If Home opens, adjust the edge gesture and follow the VoiceOver tone cues carefully.

Practice: Open the Dock and launch one app.

26.2 Switch Apps with Four Fingers

What you will learn: Move quickly among open apps on iPad/iPhone.

Before you begin: At least two apps are open.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Swipe left or right with four fingers.

  2. Listen for the newly active app.

  3. Repeat to cycle through recent apps.

What should happen: The active app changes without opening the App Switcher.

If that does not happen: If the gesture does nothing, ensure four fingers land simultaneously with spacing between them.

Practice: Cycle through three open apps.

Part 27 - Safety and Privacy

  • Use headphones when VoiceOver might speak passwords, verification codes, private messages, health information, or financial data in public.

  • Before sending a Message, Mail, or Share Sheet item, verify the recipient/destination by VoiceOver.

  • Before enabling Screen Recording, screen sharing, or mirroring, remember VoiceOver speech and visual content may expose private information depending on routing/settings.

  • Do not rely on generated image descriptions, OCR, or captions for safety-critical interpretation without verification.

  • Keep device passcode, Face ID/Touch ID, Apple Account authentication, and Find My configured appropriately.

  • Use Screen Curtain for visual privacy, but understand that it is not encryption or access control.

  • When pairing braille displays or keyboards, confirm the device name and remove old pairings you no longer use.

Part 28 - Troubleshooting

28.1 VoiceOver Stops Speaking

What you will learn: Separate speech-volume/routing problems from a frozen app.

Before you begin: VoiceOver was working and then becomes silent.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press a volume button to verify device audio is not at zero.

  2. Two-finger tap once in case speech was paused.

  3. Try touching the screen and swiping right.

  4. Check whether audio is routed to Bluetooth headphones/speaker.

  5. Ask Siri whether VoiceOver is on.

  6. If necessary, toggle VoiceOver with the Accessibility Shortcut.

What should happen: Speech resumes or the cause becomes clearer.

If that does not happen: If all device audio is silent, troubleshoot the output device before changing VoiceOver settings.

Practice: Memorize: volume > two-finger tap > touch/swipe > audio route > Siri/shortcut.

28.2 Double-Tap Activates the Wrong Item

What you will learn: Slow down selection and activation.

Before you begin: The wrong control opened.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Use a two-finger scrub to go Back/dismiss.

  2. Swipe left/right until the intended item is clearly announced.

  3. Pause briefly without touching the screen.

  4. Double-tap quickly anywhere.

What should happen: The selected item activates.

If that does not happen: If selection changes during the double-tap, keep the two taps quick and in one place.

Practice: Open three apps using swipe-to-select then double-tap.

28.3 One-Finger Swipes Do Not Move to the Next Item

What you will learn: Use a shorter, faster flick.

Before you begin: VoiceOver is on.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Keep one finger on the screen only briefly.

  2. Flick left/right rather than dragging slowly.

  3. Test in VoiceOver Practice.

What should happen: VoiceOver moves to previous/next item.

If that does not happen: A slow drag performs explore-by-touch instead of sequential navigation.

Practice: Practice ten left/right flicks.

28.4 You Are Trapped in a Screen or Dialog

What you will learn: Use Back/dismiss strategies in order.

Before you begin: A modal screen is open.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Perform a two-finger scrub.

  2. If that does not work, swipe to a Back, Cancel, Done, Close, or Dismiss button.

  3. Double-tap it.

  4. If the app is stuck, open the App Switcher and move to another app before returning.

What should happen: The dialog closes or you regain orientation.

If that does not happen: Do not repeatedly double-tap random controls; rebuild context first.

Practice: Open a harmless Settings dialog and dismiss it with scrub.

28.5 Control Center Opens When You Wanted Notifications

What you will learn: Use the edge-tone cues.

Before you begin: You are on a Face ID/no-Home-button device.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Start one finger at the top edge.

  2. Drag downward slowly.

  3. Lift at two rising tones for Control Center.

  4. Continue to the third tone for Notifications.

What should happen: You can intentionally distinguish the two screens.

If that does not happen: Practice in VoiceOver Practice/normal use until the tone cue is familiar.

Practice: Open each one twice and dismiss with scrub.

28.6 Braille Display Does Not Connect

What you will learn: Check pairing before changing braille settings.

Before you begin: A supported display is available.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Confirm the display is powered on and in the correct Apple/Bluetooth pairing mode.

  2. Open Settings > Accessibility > VoiceOver > Braille.

  3. Check whether the display appears.

  4. If it appears but will not connect, remove the stale pairing and pair again.

  5. Restart the display and iPhone/iPad if necessary.

  6. After connection, separately test output, input, and routing.

What should happen: The display connects or the failing stage is identified.

If that does not happen: Do not assume the braille table is the cause of a Bluetooth discovery problem.

Practice: Practice locating the Braille settings page.
