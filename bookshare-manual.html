Before you begin

Choose one practice task at a time. Have your usual accessibility tools running and a way to record which steps helped. Use a familiar, non-sensitive document or book for the first exercise.

Keyboard directions

A plus sign means hold the first key while tapping the final key, then release both. For three-key combinations, hold both modifiers while tapping the last key. Do not type the plus sign. “Then” means release the first command before pressing the next key.

Tab moves forward through controls; Shift+Tab moves backward. Enter activates a focused link or button; Space commonly operates buttons and checkboxes. Arrow keys move within menus and lists. Pause after each step to check where focus went. For screen-reader reading and interaction modes, see the Screen Readers manual. Phone gestures are covered in the iPhone and iPad VoiceOver and Android TalkBack manuals.

1. Prepare the correct membership

What you will learn: Separate individual reading access from organizational administration.

Exactly how to do it

  1. If a school or organization already provides your access, ask for its member sign-in instructions before making another account.
  2. For a new individual membership, open Bookshare’s Sign Up page and choose the individual route. Complete the required information.
  3. Follow the confirmation and proof-of-disability process. Review the current membership terms and any applicable fee or assistance before activation.

What should happen: You know which account to use and whether its access is ready.

If that does not happen: A created account is not necessarily an activated membership. Check the outstanding requirements before troubleshooting the reader.

Practice: Write down the account route and next required step, but do not put your password or disability paperwork in a practice worksheet.

Source: Bookshare membership setup.

2. Find the correct book

What you will learn: Check the title and edition before reading.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Sign in and locate the book search field. Search by title, author, or ISBN.
  2. Review the results. Open the intended title and compare the author and edition information with your assignment.
  3. For discovery rather than a specific title, explore Bookshare’s curated categories and lists.

What should happen: You have identified a book that fits your purpose.

If that does not happen: If the result is wrong, check spelling, try an author search, or use the ISBN. Similar titles can be different books.

Practice: Find a leisure title and a study title. Explain how you decided each was the right result.

Source: Finding books.

3. Read in a web browser

What you will learn: Start and stop reading while keeping control of navigation.

Exactly how to do it

  1. From an accessible book, activate Read Now, then the player’s Play button.
  2. Open Settings to adjust audio or display preferences.
  3. Use Book Navigation to open the table of contents and select a chapter.
  4. Pause before changing controls so the book narration does not compete with screen-reader speech.

What should happen: You can move to a chapter and listen at a comfortable pace.

If that does not happen: Check sign-in, access status, and the selected audio output. Identify the player’s actual controls instead of assuming a Space shortcut.

Practice: Read a short passage, pause, and explain it in your own words.

Source: Bookshare Reader for web.

4. Find assigned reading and mark your place

What you will learn: Return to a useful passage.

Exactly how to do it

  1. With organizational member access, check Assigned Books or Reading Lists for the material provided to you.
  2. Open the book with Read Now. Locate the assigned chapter using Book Navigation.
  3. Create a bookmark at a passage you want to revisit, then practice returning to it.

What should happen: You can distinguish the assignment location from your position inside the book.

If that does not happen: If a title is missing, confirm which member account received it. Ask the assigning instructor to check the assignment rather than sharing account credentials.

Practice: Choose a passage, record a short description, and use your bookmark to return to it.

Source: Assignments and bookmarks.

5. Choose a reading device

What you will learn: Match the reading tool to your access needs.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Review Bookshare’s supported reading tools. Bookshare Reader is available for browsers, iOS, Android, and Alexa; partner tools provide other options.
  2. Choose one tool and sign in through its documented Bookshare connection.
  3. Try a short book on that device before depending on it for a long assignment.

What should happen: You have a workable reading method for your device.

If that does not happen: Do not assume all tools use identical controls or support every format. Check the selected tool’s instructions.

Practice: Compare listening, enlarged text, or braille according to your needs. Explain which method you would choose for leisure versus study.

Source: Supported reading tools.

6. Build a recovery routine

What you will learn: Describe an access problem clearly.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Separate sign-in trouble, missing book access, navigation trouble, and missing sound.
  2. Write down the book title, device, reading tool, and exact error. Do not include passwords.
  3. Use the Bookshare Help and Learning Center for that specific problem. For an organizational account, involve the instructor who manages access.

What should happen: Your support request describes something another person can investigate.

If that does not happen: Avoid deleting a book, reinstalling the app, or creating duplicate accounts before identifying the problem.

Practice: Pretend a book opens but has no sound. List what you would check before concluding that the book is unavailable.

Source: Bookshare help.

PurposeAction

These are navigation routines, not universal Bookshare single-key shortcuts. Confirm the focused control and pause narration while exploring.

Independent practice

Choose any activity; there is no required order or passing score.

  1. Identify your membership route and whether access is active.
  2. Find a book by title and verify the author.
  3. Find a chapter and summarize one paragraph.
  4. Return to a marked passage.
  5. Explain how a sign-in problem differs from a playback problem.

After each task, record whether you completed it independently, needed a prompt, or want another attempt. Repeat the difficult step instead of restarting everything.
