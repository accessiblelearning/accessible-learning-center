Official Zoom Workplace Getting Started Guide

Purpose: This manual is written directly to the person learning Zoom. It assumes you may be opening Zoom for the first time. Every procedure explains where to go, exactly what to click or press, what should happen, and what to do if the result is different.

Current interface: Zoom states that the Zoom Workplace desktop interface was updated in version 6.7.0. This manual uses that current interface as its reference while explaining alternatives when a toolbar item is under More or has been customized.

Important: Meeting features can be disabled or restricted by the host, account administrator, organization, license, or meeting policy. If a control described here is missing, that does not always mean you are doing something wrong.

How to Use This Manual

  • Work through Parts 1-8 first if Zoom is completely new to you.

  • Do one action at a time, then stop and confirm what changed.

  • Tab moves forward through many buttons and fields. Shift+Tab moves backward.

  • Enter or Spacebar activates many focused controls.

  • Escape closes many menus, dialogs, and temporary panels.

  • Windows and Mac shortcuts are shown separately when they differ.

  • Screen-reader users should listen to both the control name and the control type before activating it.

  • Zoom lets users customize toolbar placement, and hosts/admins can restrict features. Use the control name rather than assuming it is always in the same position.

  • Use Zoom Settings > Keyboard shortcuts to review the exact shortcuts available in your installed version.

Part 1 - What Zoom Is

Zoom Workplace is a collaboration application that can include meetings, team chat, phone, calendar, mail, whiteboard, and other tools depending on your account. This manual focuses primarily on the features most people need to participate independently in Zoom meetings.

  • A Zoom meeting is an online session where participants can use audio, video, chat, captions, reactions, screen sharing, and other features.

  • A host starts or controls the meeting. A co-host can be given many host controls.

  • A participant joins the meeting and can use the controls allowed by the host.

  • A waiting room can hold participants before the host admits them.

  • A meeting ID identifies a meeting. Some meetings also require a passcode.

  • A join link can contain enough information to open the correct meeting directly.

  • Zoom Workplace desktop app is different from the Zoom web portal. The desktop app is used for meetings and local app settings; the web portal contains many account-level settings.

Part 2 - Install, Open, and Sign In

2.1 Open Zoom Workplace on Windows

What you will learn: Open Zoom using only the keyboard.

Before you begin: Zoom Workplace must already be installed.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press the Windows key.

  2. Type Zoom Workplace.

  3. Wait for the Zoom Workplace app to appear in Windows search.

  4. Press Enter.

  5. Wait for the Zoom window to open.

  6. If the first screen gives you Sign in, Join a meeting, or Sign up, choose Sign in if you have an account and want access to your scheduled meetings/settings.

  7. Use Tab to move to Sign in and press Enter if necessary.

  8. Enter the account information requested by Zoom or your organization.

  9. Complete any browser-based, single-sign-on, password, or verification prompts.

  10. Return to the Zoom Workplace app and wait until its main window finishes loading.

What should happen: The main Zoom Workplace window opens with your account signed in.

If that does not happen: If Zoom does not appear in Windows search, it may not be installed. If your organization uses SSO, make sure you choose the correct sign-in method rather than repeatedly entering a personal Zoom password.

Practice: Close Zoom, reopen it from Windows Search, and identify whether you are signed in.

2.2 Open Zoom Workplace on a Mac

What you will learn: Open Zoom from Spotlight.

Before you begin: Zoom Workplace must already be installed.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press Command+Space.

  2. Type Zoom Workplace.

  3. Press Return.

  4. Wait for the app to open.

  5. Use Tab or VoiceOver navigation to move to Sign in if needed.

  6. Choose the correct sign-in method.

  7. Complete password, browser, SSO, or verification steps.

  8. Return to Zoom after authentication.

What should happen: Zoom Workplace opens and displays the signed-in app.

If that does not happen: If Spotlight cannot find Zoom Workplace, check the Applications folder or install the current Zoom Workplace app.

Practice: Quit and reopen Zoom once.

Part 3 - Learn the Zoom Workplace Window

Zoom's main app window can include Home, Meetings/Calendar, Team Chat, Contacts, Whiteboards, and additional products. Zoom notes that the desktop interface changed in version 6.7.0, so the exact navigation bar may differ from older instructions.

  • Navigation bar: switches among Zoom products and work areas.

  • Home: shows upcoming meetings and common actions such as Join, New meeting, or Schedule.

  • Meetings or Calendar: displays scheduled meetings/events depending on your account integration.

  • Team Chat: displays Zoom chat conversations and channels.

  • Profile picture/account menu: opens status, account controls, Settings, and update options.

  • Settings: contains Audio, Video, Accessibility, Keyboard shortcuts, and other preferences.

  • Meeting window: opens separately when you start or join a meeting.

3.1 Explore the Zoom Window with Tab

What you will learn: Learn where keyboard focus moves before activating controls.

Before you begin: Zoom Workplace is open.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press Tab once.

  2. Listen to or visually identify the focused item.

  3. Press Tab again.

  4. Continue through several controls.

  5. Press Shift+Tab to move backward.

  6. When focus enters a list, try Up Arrow and Down Arrow to move within the list.

  7. Press Escape if you enter a temporary menu or pane that you do not want to use.

  8. Continue until you can identify the navigation area, central content, and profile/settings controls.

What should happen: You can move through the app without clicking random controls.

If that does not happen: If Tab appears to stop moving, try Escape once, then Tab again. If focus is inside a list, use arrow keys.

Practice: Explore the Home screen for two minutes without starting or joining a meeting.

4.1 Find Zoom's Current Keyboard Shortcuts

What you will learn: Use Zoom's own Settings to verify a command.

Before you begin: Be signed into the Zoom Workplace desktop app.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open your profile/account menu.

  2. Choose Settings.

  3. Move to Keyboard shortcuts.

  4. Move through the list of shortcut actions.

  5. Find the action you want, such as Mute/unmute, Start/stop video, Participants, Chat, or Share screen.

  6. Read the key combination assigned to it.

  7. If Zoom allows editing and you intentionally want a different shortcut, change it carefully.

  8. Close Settings when finished.

What should happen: You can verify the shortcut rather than relying on memory.

If that does not happen: If Keyboard shortcuts is not visible, update Zoom Workplace and make sure you are using the desktop app rather than a limited browser interface.

Practice: Verify the shortcuts for audio, video, chat, participants, and screen sharing.

Part 5 - Join a Meeting from a Link

5.1 Join from an Email or Calendar Link

What you will learn: Join the correct meeting and make deliberate audio/video choices before entering.

Before you begin: Have the meeting invitation available.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open the invitation email or calendar event.

  2. Move to the Zoom join link.

  3. Activate the link.

  4. If a browser asks whether to open Zoom Workplace, choose Open Zoom Meetings/Zoom Workplace.

  5. If Zoom is not installed, the browser may offer to download it or may provide an option to join from the browser.

  6. Wait for the Zoom pre-join screen.

  7. Read the meeting name if shown so you know you are joining the correct session.

  8. Check your display name. Edit it if the meeting allows and if it is incorrect.

  9. Check the microphone/audio choice.

  10. Check the camera/video choice.

  11. When you are ready, activate Join.

  12. If the meeting uses a waiting room, wait for the host to admit you.

What should happen: You enter the correct meeting or waiting room.

If that does not happen: If the link opens the wrong account or browser profile, close the meeting and reopen the invitation using the account expected by the organizer. If Zoom says the meeting does not exist, verify that the link is complete and current.

Practice: Use Zoom's test meeting at zoom.us/test before an important first meeting.

Part 6 - Join with Meeting ID and Passcode

6.1 Join without Using a Link

What you will learn: Enter a meeting ID manually.

Before you begin: Have the meeting ID and passcode from the organizer.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open Zoom Workplace.

  2. On the initial or Home screen, activate Join.

  3. Move to the Meeting ID field.

  4. Type the meeting ID exactly. Spaces can be ignored if Zoom formats the number automatically.

  5. Move to the display-name field if shown and enter the name you want participants to see.

  6. Choose whether to connect without audio or turn off your video before joining if those options are shown.

  7. Activate Join.

  8. If prompted, type the meeting passcode exactly.

  9. Activate Join meeting.

  10. Wait for the meeting or waiting room.

What should happen: You enter the meeting or waiting room using the ID and passcode.

If that does not happen: If Zoom says the passcode is wrong, recheck uppercase/lowercase characters and make sure you did not confuse similar characters such as 0/O or 1/l.

Practice: Practice opening the Join dialog and identifying every field without actually joining.

Part 7 - Test Audio, Microphone, Speaker, and Camera

Zoom provides a test meeting at zoom.us/test. Zoom recommends using it to become familiar with meeting controls and test your microphone and speakers before a real meeting.

7.1 Test Your Speaker

What you will learn: Confirm that meeting audio will come through the intended device.

Before you begin: Connect your headphones, speakers, or headset.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open Zoom Settings from your profile/account menu.

  2. Open Audio settings.

  3. Find the Speaker device selection.

  4. Read the currently selected device.

  5. Open the device list if you need to change it.

  6. Choose your desired headphones or speakers.

  7. Activate Test Speaker.

  8. Listen for the test sound.

  9. Adjust the Zoom or system output volume if needed.

What should happen: You hear the Zoom test sound through the intended device.

If that does not happen: If you hear nothing, check system volume, mute state, cable/Bluetooth connection, and whether Zoom selected the wrong output device.

Practice: Switch temporarily to a second available speaker device and then restore your preferred device.

7.2 Test Your Microphone

What you will learn: Confirm that Zoom can hear you.

Before you begin: Connect the microphone or headset you intend to use.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open Zoom Settings.

  2. Open Audio settings.

  3. Find the Microphone selection.

  4. Read the selected microphone.

  5. Choose the correct microphone if necessary.

  6. Speak at a normal level.

  7. Watch or listen for Zoom's input-level/test feedback.

  8. Use Test Mic if available and follow the recording/playback procedure.

  9. Adjust the input volume only if needed.

What should happen: Zoom detects your voice and, when using the test feature, plays it back.

If that does not happen: If Zoom detects no voice, check the headset's physical mute switch, Windows/macOS microphone privacy permission, selected device, and hardware connection.

Practice: Say a full sentence at normal speaking volume and verify the microphone detects it.

7.3 Check Your Camera Before Joining

What you will learn: Confirm which camera Zoom will use.

Before you begin: Connect or uncover the camera.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open Zoom Settings.

  2. Open Video & effects or Video settings.

  3. Read the selected camera name.

  4. Choose the correct camera if more than one exists.

  5. Check the video preview if you use vision, or confirm camera status through screen-reader labels/physical privacy indicators.

  6. Close Settings.

What should happen: Zoom is set to the intended camera.

If that does not happen: If the camera is unavailable, close other apps that may be using it and check operating-system camera privacy permission.

Practice: Identify how to switch cameras without entering a real meeting.

Part 8 - Understand the Meeting Window

When you join a meeting, the meeting controls toolbar contains the controls you use most. Zoom allows toolbar customization, so a control can be directly on the toolbar or inside More.

  • Audio: mute/unmute and select microphone/speaker.

  • Video: start/stop video and choose camera/effects.

  • Participants: view who is in the meeting.

  • Chat: open in-meeting messages.

  • Share: share a screen, window, whiteboard, or other content if permitted.

  • React: raise hand, reactions, and non-verbal feedback.

  • More: additional controls such as captions, recording, settings, and features not placed directly on your toolbar.

  • Leave: exit the meeting. Hosts may instead see End with choices to end for everyone or leave.

Important: A host or administrator can prevent participants from using features such as screen sharing, chat, video, captions, recording, or reactions.

Part 9 - Mute and Unmute

9.1 Mute or Unmute Yourself

What you will learn: Control whether other participants can hear your microphone.

Before you begin: Be inside a meeting and connected to computer audio.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Windows: press Alt+A. Mac: press Command+Shift+A.

  2. Listen for Zoom's audio status announcement or inspect the microphone icon.

  3. If you were unmuted, you should now be muted.

  4. Press the same shortcut again.

  5. If the host allows you to unmute, you should now be unmuted.

  6. When muted and the Zoom meeting window has focus, you can hold the Spacebar temporarily to speak if push-to-talk is enabled/available; release Spacebar to return to mute.

What should happen: Your microphone toggles between muted and unmuted.

If that does not happen: If Zoom says the host has prevented you from unmuting, you must wait for the host to allow it. If Alt+A/Command+Shift+A does not work, locate the Audio button and activate it.

Practice: Practice toggling twice and state whether your microphone is muted at the end.

Part 10 - Start and Stop Video

10.1 Turn Your Camera On or Off

What you will learn: Control whether your camera video is being sent.

Before you begin: Be inside a meeting.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Windows: press Alt+V. Mac: press Command+Shift+V.

  2. Listen for the video status or check the camera icon.

  3. Press the same shortcut again to reverse the state.

  4. If the host has disabled participant video, Zoom may prevent you from starting it.

  5. To choose a different camera, open the menu next to the Video button and select the desired camera.

What should happen: Video changes between on and off when permitted.

If that does not happen: If the camera does not start, check camera privacy permission, camera selection, hardware privacy shutter, and whether another app is using the camera.

Practice: Turn video on, identify the selected camera, then turn video off.

Part 11 - Participants

11.1 Open the Participants Panel

What you will learn: Find out who is in the meeting and review participant status.

Before you begin: Be inside a meeting.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Windows: press Alt+U. Mac: press Command+U.

  2. Wait for the Participants panel to open.

  3. Press Tab until focus moves into the participants list if it is not already there.

  4. Use Up Arrow/Down Arrow to move through participant names when supported.

  5. Listen for status information such as muted, host, co-host, raised hand, or other available labels.

  6. Press Alt+U on Windows or Command+U on Mac again, or activate the panel close control, to close the panel.

What should happen: You can review participant names and close the panel.

If that does not happen: If the shortcut does not open the panel, Tab through the meeting toolbar to Participants and activate it.

Practice: Open Participants, identify three names/statuses, and close the panel.

Part 12 - In-Meeting Chat

12.1 Open Chat and Send a Message

What you will learn: Send a text message during a meeting.

Before you begin: Chat must be allowed by the host/account.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Windows: press Alt+H. Mac: press Command+Shift+H.

  2. Wait for the Chat panel.

  3. Move to the message-entry field with Tab if necessary.

  4. Check the recipient selector if the meeting allows private messages. Make sure you know whether you are sending to Everyone or one person.

  5. Type your message.

  6. Press Enter/Return to send.

  7. Move to the message history with Shift+Tab or the appropriate navigation controls if you want to read previous messages.

  8. Use arrow keys or screen-reader navigation to read messages.

  9. Close Chat with the shortcut or panel close control.

What should happen: The message appears in the selected chat destination.

If that does not happen: If Chat is missing or you cannot send, the host or administrator may have restricted chat. Do not assume a keyboard problem.

Practice: Send a short test message in a meeting where you have permission.

Privacy: Always confirm the chat recipient before sending. A message intended for one person can be embarrassing or sensitive if sent to Everyone.

Part 13 - Captions and Accessibility

Zoom Accessibility settings can change chat display size, caption font size/color/font/position, automatic video dimming for flashing content, GIF animation behavior, and screen-reader alerts.

13.1 Turn On Captions in a Meeting

What you will learn: Display spoken meeting content as captions when available.

Before you begin: Be inside a meeting where captions are enabled/allowed.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Move to More on the meeting toolbar if Show Captions is not already visible.

  2. Open More.

  3. Move through the menu until Show Captions is focused.

  4. Activate Show Captions.

  5. If Zoom asks for a language or caption choice, select the spoken language used in the meeting.

  6. Wait for captions to appear.

  7. To stop displaying captions, return to the same control and choose the appropriate hide/stop option.

What should happen: Captions appear as people speak.

If that does not happen: If Show Captions is unavailable, the feature may be disabled by account policy or not enabled for the meeting.

Practice: Turn captions on in a test meeting and find where they appear.

13.2 Make Captions Easier to Read

What you will learn: Change how captions look on your device.

Before you begin: Be signed into Zoom Workplace.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open your profile/account menu.

  2. Choose Settings.

  3. Open Accessibility.

  4. Find the Closed captioning section.

  5. Adjust Font size.

  6. Choose caption color/background combinations if available.

  7. Choose the font type if desired.

  8. Choose whether captions are pinned at the bottom or shown as a movable/resizable overlay, if your version offers that option.

  9. Enable Always show captions in meetings and webinars if you want Zoom to display captions automatically whenever they are available.

  10. Close Settings.

What should happen: Captions use your chosen display preferences.

If that does not happen: If a setting is unavailable, update Zoom Workplace and verify your platform supports it.

Practice: Increase caption size, close Settings, then return and restore your preferred size.

13.3 Configure Screen Reader Alerts

What you will learn: Control which Zoom meeting events your screen reader announces.

Before you begin: Use a screen reader and be signed in.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open Zoom Settings.

  2. Open Accessibility.

  3. Find Screen reader alerts.

  4. Move through the available meeting-action, chat, and permission alerts.

  5. Enable alerts you need.

  6. Disable alerts that are too repetitive.

  7. Use Restore if you want to return to Zoom's default alert choices.

What should happen: Your screen reader announces the selected Zoom events.

If that does not happen: If Zoom feels overly verbose, reduce only the specific alerts that are unnecessary rather than disabling all feedback.

Practice: Review the alert list and identify at least three announcements that are useful to you.

Part 14 - Raise Hand, Reactions, and Non-Verbal Feedback

14.1 Raise and Lower Your Hand

What you will learn: Signal that you want to speak.

Before you begin: Be inside a meeting where reactions/non-verbal feedback are allowed.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Windows: press Alt+Y. Mac: press Option+Y.

  2. Listen for or inspect the raised-hand status.

  3. Wait for the host to acknowledge you.

  4. When you no longer need the hand raised, press the same shortcut again.

What should happen: Your hand status changes between raised and lowered.

If that does not happen: If the shortcut does not work, open React and choose Raise Hand. The host/admin may have disabled some reaction features.

Practice: Raise your hand, verify it is raised, then lower it.

14.2 Send a Reaction

What you will learn: Use a temporary emoji reaction without interrupting the speaker.

Before you begin: Be inside a meeting where reactions are enabled.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Move to React on the meeting toolbar.

  2. Open it.

  3. Choose a reaction such as Thumbs Up, Clapping Hands, Heart, or another available emoji.

  4. Activate the reaction.

  5. Wait for the reaction to appear temporarily.

  6. Open React again if you need to choose non-verbal feedback such as Yes or No where available.

What should happen: The selected reaction appears according to the meeting's settings.

If that does not happen: If only a limited set appears, the account or meeting may use selected emojis rather than the full emoji set.

Practice: Send a Thumbs Up reaction in a practice meeting.

Part 15 - Share Your Screen or a Window

15.1 Share a Specific Window

What you will learn: Share only the application you intend to show.

Before you begin: Close private information before sharing.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open the application/window you want to share before opening the share picker.

  2. Return to the Zoom meeting.

  3. Windows: press Alt+S. Mac: press Command+Shift+S.

  4. Wait for the Share Screen/Share Content picker.

  5. Move through the available screens, windows, tabs, whiteboards, or other share sources.

  6. Choose the specific window you intend to share rather than the entire desktop whenever practical.

  7. If you are sharing video/audio content, review any Share sound or optimize video options before starting.

  8. Activate Share.

  9. Wait for Zoom to indicate that sharing has started.

  10. Navigate to the shared application and present your content.

  11. To stop sharing, move to the floating share toolbar and activate Stop Share, or use the appropriate shortcut/control.

What should happen: Participants see only the chosen content until you stop sharing.

If that does not happen: If Zoom does not offer screen/window choices, check host permission, account policy, and operating-system screen-recording/privacy permission. On macOS, screen recording permission may be required.

Practice: Share a non-sensitive window in a test meeting, verify sharing, then stop.

Privacy: Before sharing your whole desktop, close email, chat pop-ups, password managers, personal notifications, client records, and anything else that should not be exposed.

Part 16 - Leave a Meeting

16.1 Leave as a Participant

What you will learn: Exit the meeting without ending it for everyone.

Before you begin: Be inside a meeting.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Move to the Leave button on the meeting toolbar.

  2. Activate Leave.

  3. If Zoom asks you to confirm, choose Leave Meeting.

  4. Wait for the meeting window to close.

What should happen: You leave while the meeting can continue for others.

If that does not happen: If you are the host, Zoom may show End rather than a simple Leave button. Read the choices carefully before selecting End meeting for all.

Practice: Join a private test meeting and locate the Leave control before doing anything else.

Part 17 - Schedule a Meeting

17.1 Schedule from Zoom Workplace

What you will learn: Create a meeting with a date, time, and meeting options.

Before you begin: Be signed into Zoom Workplace.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open Home.

  2. Activate Schedule.

  3. Wait for the scheduling window.

  4. Enter the meeting topic/title.

  5. Set the date.

  6. Set the start time.

  7. Set the duration/end time as provided by your Zoom version.

  8. If you need a recurring meeting, open Repeat/Recurrence and choose Daily, Weekly, Monthly, Every weekday, Custom, or another available pattern.

  9. Review security choices such as passcode and Waiting Room.

  10. Choose whether host video and participant video should start on or off if those options are available.

  11. Choose audio options appropriate to your organization.

  12. Review calendar integration if Zoom will create an Outlook/Google/Zoom Calendar event.

  13. Activate Save.

  14. If an external calendar event opens, add invitees and send the calendar invitation.

What should happen: The meeting appears in your upcoming meetings/calendar.

If that does not happen: If Save is unavailable, verify that date/time and any required security settings are valid.

Practice: Schedule a practice meeting for a future day, then open its details.

Part 18 - Start a Meeting You Host

18.1 Start Your Scheduled Meeting

What you will learn: Enter your meeting as the host.

Before you begin: Be signed into the same Zoom account that scheduled the meeting.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open Home or Meetings/Calendar.

  2. Find the scheduled meeting.

  3. Read the title/date/time to confirm it is the correct meeting.

  4. Activate Start.

  5. Wait for the meeting window.

  6. Connect audio.

  7. Verify microphone and video state.

  8. If participants are in the waiting room, open Participants and admit them when ready.

What should happen: You enter with host controls.

If that does not happen: If Zoom treats you as a participant instead of host, confirm you are signed into the account that scheduled the meeting.

Practice: Start a private practice meeting and locate the Security and Participants controls.

Part 19 - Invite Others

19.1 Copy a Meeting Invitation Before the Meeting

What you will learn: Copy the join information so you can send it by email or message.

Before you begin: Have a scheduled meeting.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open the meeting card/details.

  2. Open the More/ellipsis menu if Copy invitation is not directly shown.

  3. Choose Copy invitation.

  4. Open the email or messaging application you want to use.

  5. Paste the copied invitation.

  6. Review the meeting title, date/time, join link, meeting ID, and passcode before sending.

  7. Send it only to the intended recipients.

What should happen: The recipient receives the Zoom join information.

If that does not happen: If Copy invitation is missing, open the meeting details or use the Zoom web portal to copy the invitation.

Practice: Copy a practice invitation into a blank document and identify the join link, meeting ID, and passcode.

19.2 Invite Someone During a Meeting

What you will learn: Invite another person while the meeting is already running.

Before you begin: Be the host or have permission to invite.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open Participants.

  2. At the bottom of the Participants panel, activate Invite.

  3. Choose a supported invitation method such as Contacts, Email, Copy invite link, or Copy invitation.

  4. If using Copy invite link/invitation, paste it into a message to the intended person.

  5. Close the Invite window when finished.

What should happen: The invited person receives usable join information.

If that does not happen: If some invite methods are missing, your account may not have those integrations enabled.

Practice: Open the Invite window in a practice meeting and identify at least two invitation methods.

Part 20 - Host and Security Controls

Hosts can control participant permissions. Zoom documents options such as allowing or preventing screen sharing, chat, renaming, unmuting, starting video, whiteboards, and recording, depending on account and meeting configuration.

20.1 Review Meeting Security Without Changing Anything

What you will learn: Understand where host controls are located.

Before you begin: Start a meeting as host.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Find and open Security on the meeting toolbar, or open the host controls area used by your current Zoom layout.

  2. Move through the available options one at a time.

  3. Identify whether Waiting Room is enabled.

  4. Identify whether participants can Share Screen.

  5. Identify whether participants can Chat.

  6. Identify whether participants can Rename.

  7. Identify whether participants can Unmute themselves.

  8. Identify whether participants can Start video.

  9. Close the menu without changing anything.

What should happen: You know which participant capabilities the meeting currently allows.

If that does not happen: If Security is missing, you may not be host/co-host or your Zoom layout may place the controls under another host menu.

Practice: Review the security menu before hosting an important meeting.

Part 21 - Waiting Room

21.1 Admit a Participant from the Waiting Room

What you will learn: Let the correct person enter your meeting.

Before you begin: Be host/co-host in a meeting with Waiting Room enabled.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open Participants.

  2. Find the Waiting Room section.

  3. Move to the person's name.

  4. Read the full display name before admitting them.

  5. Activate Admit.

  6. Wait for the person to move into the active participant list.

What should happen: The selected person joins the meeting.

If that does not happen: If you do not recognize the person, do not admit them until you can verify their identity through an appropriate method.

Practice: Use a second test device/account if available to practice the waiting-room flow.

Part 22 - Breakout Rooms

Breakout Rooms divide a Zoom meeting into smaller sessions. The host can assign rooms manually or automatically; participants may be asked to join a room, and some meetings allow self-selection.

22.1 Join a Breakout Room as a Participant

What you will learn: Move from the main meeting into an assigned breakout room.

Before you begin: The host has opened breakout rooms and assigned or allowed you to choose a room.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Wait for Zoom's breakout-room invitation prompt.

  2. Read the room name if shown.

  3. Activate Join.

  4. Wait for the breakout-room meeting area to load.

  5. Use audio, video, chat, and other permitted controls normally.

  6. When you want to return and the meeting allows it, activate Leave.

  7. Choose Leave Breakout Room rather than Leave Meeting if you want to return to the main session.

What should happen: You enter the breakout room and can return to the main meeting.

If that does not happen: If no invitation appears, the host may not have assigned you yet. If you accidentally choose Leave Meeting, you may exit the entire meeting.

Practice: Before joining a real breakout session, remember the difference between Leave Breakout Room and Leave Meeting.

Part 23 - Recording

Recording availability depends on account, host permission, organization policy, and local laws/policies. Participants may need permission to record.

23.1 Recognize Recording Controls and Notifications

What you will learn: Understand what happens when recording starts.

Before you begin: Be in a meeting where recording is permitted.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Listen for Zoom's recording notification or inspect the recording indicator.

  2. If you are host and recording is allowed, locate Record on the toolbar or under More.

  3. Choose the available recording method, such as computer/local or cloud, according to your account.

  4. Follow any disclosure/consent requirements that apply to your organization and jurisdiction.

  5. To stop, return to the recording control and choose Stop Recording.

  6. After the meeting, allow Zoom time to process or convert the recording if required.

What should happen: You can tell whether recording is active and stop it intentionally.

If that does not happen: If Record is missing, your account, administrator, or host may not allow it.

Practice: Locate the recording control in a practice meeting without starting a recording unless you have permission.

Part 27 - Windows Screen Reader Use

Part 29 - Zoom Web App

Zoom can run in a browser when allowed. Browser behavior and shortcuts can differ from the desktop app, and some features may be limited.

  • Choose Join from your browser if the meeting page offers it and you do not want/cannot use the desktop app.

  • Grant microphone/camera browser permissions only when needed.

  • Use the browser's normal Tab/Shift+Tab navigation plus Zoom Web App controls.

  • Be aware that browser shortcuts can conflict with Zoom keyboard shortcuts.

  • For the most complete and predictable accessibility experience, use a current supported browser and Zoom's current Web App documentation.

Part 30 - Zoom Mobile App

The Zoom Workplace mobile app is available on iOS and Android. The basic meeting concepts are the same, but controls are touch-based.

  • Tap the screen during a meeting if controls have hidden themselves.

  • Use Mute/Unmute, Start/Stop Video, Participants, More, Share, and Leave controls.

  • VoiceOver users on iPhone/iPad move with swipe left/right and activate with double-tap.

  • TalkBack users on Android move with swipe gestures and activate with double-tap.

  • Mobile caption/chat text size can depend on the device's accessibility/font settings.

Part 31 - Troubleshooting

31.1 Nobody Can Hear You

What you will learn: Find whether the cause is mute state, device choice, permission, or hardware.

Before you begin: Be in a meeting or open Audio Settings.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press Alt+A on Windows or Command+Shift+A on Mac and confirm whether you are muted.

  2. Check whether the host has prevented participants from unmuting.

  3. Open Zoom Settings > Audio.

  4. Confirm the intended microphone is selected.

  5. Speak and check Zoom's input feedback.

  6. Check the microphone's physical mute switch.

  7. Check Windows/macOS microphone privacy permission for Zoom Workplace.

  8. Reconnect the headset/microphone.

  9. Use Test Mic.

What should happen: Zoom detects your voice and other participants can hear you when unmuted.

If that does not happen: If the operating system also cannot detect the microphone, fix the hardware/system issue before changing more Zoom settings.

Practice: Run this checklist before an important meeting.

31.2 You Cannot Hear Other People

What you will learn: Troubleshoot output audio.

Before you begin: Meeting audio should be active.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Check the computer's volume and mute state.

  2. Open Zoom Settings > Audio.

  3. Confirm the intended Speaker device.

  4. Activate Test Speaker.

  5. If using Bluetooth headphones, verify they are connected to the computer.

  6. Check whether you actually joined computer audio in the meeting.

  7. Leave and rejoin computer audio if necessary.

  8. Try another speaker/headset if available.

What should happen: You hear meeting and test audio.

If that does not happen: If other applications also have no sound, troubleshoot the operating system or output device rather than Zoom alone.

Practice: Verify the speaker device before your next meeting.

31.3 Your Camera Will Not Start

What you will learn: Check video problems in a sensible order.

Before you begin: Your camera is connected/uncovered.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press Alt+V or Command+Shift+V.

  2. Check whether the host has prevented participants from starting video.

  3. Open Zoom Settings > Video & effects.

  4. Confirm the correct camera.

  5. Close other apps that may be using the camera.

  6. Check the physical camera privacy shutter/switch.

  7. Check Windows/macOS camera privacy permission.

  8. Restart Zoom if necessary.

What should happen: The camera becomes available when permitted.

If that does not happen: If no app can use the camera, troubleshoot the hardware/system first.

Practice: Identify the selected camera and physical privacy control.

31.4 You Cannot Share Your Screen

What you will learn: Determine whether the problem is host permission, account policy, or operating-system permission.

Before you begin: Be inside a meeting.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Try Alt+S/Command+Shift+S or activate Share.

  2. If Zoom says only the host can share, ask the host to allow participant sharing.

  3. If Share is missing, it may be restricted by admin/meeting policy.

  4. On macOS, check Screen Recording/Screen & System Audio Recording privacy permission for Zoom.

  5. Restart Zoom after changing required system permissions.

  6. Try sharing a single window.

What should happen: The share picker opens and your selected content can be shared.

If that does not happen: If the host/admin has disabled sharing, you cannot override that restriction from your device.

Practice: Practice finding the Share control even when you do not actually start sharing.

31.5 You Are in the Waiting Room

What you will learn: Recognize that Zoom is working and you are waiting for admission.

Before you begin: You have joined a meeting that uses Waiting Room.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Read the waiting-room message.

  2. Do not repeatedly leave and rejoin unless instructed.

  3. Confirm that your display name identifies you clearly enough for the host to recognize you.

  4. Wait for the host to admit you.

  5. If the wait is unusually long, contact the organizer through another agreed communication method.

What should happen: You enter when the host admits you.

If that does not happen: If the host does not recognize your display name, you may remain waiting longer. Use an appropriate recognizable name before joining when possible.

Practice: Learn to distinguish a waiting-room message from a connection-error message.

31.6 Zoom Says the Meeting ID or Passcode Is Invalid

What you will learn: Correct invitation-entry problems.

Before you begin: Have the original invitation.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Return to the original invitation rather than relying on a handwritten number if possible.

  2. Re-enter the Meeting ID.

  3. Re-enter the passcode exactly.

  4. Check similar characters such as O/0 and I/l/1.

  5. Make sure you are joining the correct occurrence of a recurring meeting.

  6. Try the full join link from the invitation.

  7. Contact the organizer if the invitation may have been changed or cancelled.

What should happen: You join successfully or confirm that the meeting information itself is outdated.

If that does not happen: If the link also fails, the meeting may have ended, been deleted, or been changed by the host.

Practice: Compare a meeting ID/passcode with the original invitation before entering it manually.
