What generative AI does

Generative AI predicts and creates text or other content from patterns in training data and the information supplied in a conversation. It can help brainstorm, organize, explain, summarize, and draft. It does not understand a situation the way a person does, and it can produce confident but incorrect or invented information.

Use AI as an assistant, not as the final decision-maker. A person remains responsible for checking accuracy, appropriateness, accessibility, and compliance with workplace rules.

A reliable prompt pattern

  1. Task: State exactly what you want.
  2. Context: Explain the audience and purpose using nonprivate information.
  3. Constraints: Give length, tone, reading level, deadline, or other limits.
  4. Format: Request headings, bullets, a numbered procedure, or a simple table.
  5. Checks: Ask the tool to identify uncertainty and provide sources when appropriate.

Continue the conversation with specific follow-ups. For example: “Shorten this to 150 words,” “Explain the second step,” or “Which statements need verification?”

Verify before using the result

  • Open original sources instead of trusting an AI summary or citation.
  • Confirm names, dates, quotations, calculations, contact details, laws, policies, and software instructions.
  • Prefer current primary sources from the organization responsible for the information.
  • Compare important claims with a second reliable source.
  • Test instructions safely before giving them to another person.
  • Rewrite the final result in your own voice and keep a record of corrections.

Privacy and workplace rules

Do not enter client records, medical or disability details, passwords, financial account information, private addresses, confidential workplace material, copyrighted documents you are not permitted to share, or information that identifies another person. Use fictional or public information in these lessons.

Follow the employer’s approved-tool, retention, disclosure, and human-review requirements. A personal free account is not automatically approved for organizational data. Never allow AI to make an employment, benefit, medical, legal, financial, safety, or accommodation decision for a person.

Accessible output

Ask for descriptive headings, short paragraphs, meaningful link text, numbered steps, and simple tables. Ask for image descriptions when an image matters, but verify the description with a trusted person when accuracy is important. Avoid layouts that communicate meaning only through color, position, or visual formatting.

JAWS, Narrator, and NVDA

AI interfaces change often. Use Tab and Shift+Tab to move among controls, Enter or Space to activate a control, and the screen reader’s heading, landmark, link, button, edit-field, and browse-mode commands to review the page. Use browser Find to locate words such as “Sources,” “Copy,” or part of your prompt.

  • JAWS: H moves by heading, B by button, E by edit field, Insert+F7 opens links, and Insert+F5 opens form fields.
  • NVDA: H moves by heading, B by button, E by edit field, NVDA+F7 opens the Elements List, and NVDA+Space switches browse and focus modes.
  • Narrator: Use Caps Lock or Insert with arrow keys for Scan Mode navigation; press Caps Lock+Space to toggle Scan Mode when needed.

After sending a prompt, allow time for the response to finish. Move by heading or paragraph and confirm whether focus changed. Product-specific keyboard behavior may change after updates.

Basic procedure

1. What Generative AI Can and Cannot Do

What you will learn: Explain three appropriate AI uses and three tasks that still require human judgment.

Before you begin: Use a free or approved AI service and only fictional or public information. Do not enter private workplace, client, medical, financial, or account information.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open the AI Fundamentals and Responsible Use manual and review the prompting, verification, privacy, and screen-reader sections.
  2. Open an available AI tool in a regular browser tab. If an account or feature is unavailable, study the sample prompt and write the expected process instead.
  3. Navigate by headings and form controls. Find the prompt edit field and confirm its label before typing.
  4. Use this practice prompt or adapt it without adding private information:
    Act as an accessible workplace skills coach. Help me explain three appropriate AI uses and three tasks that still require human judgment. Use fictional or public information only. Organize the response with descriptive headings and numbered steps. Identify anything I must verify.
  5. Read the complete response by heading, paragraph, list item, and link. Do not assume the first answer is correct.
  6. Send at least one follow-up prompt that improves clarity, accuracy, accessibility, or completeness.
  7. Verify important claims using the original source or another authoritative source. Record unsupported statements and corrections.
  8. Edit the final result in your own words. Remove generic, inaccurate, biased, or unnecessary content.
  9. Create a short process note naming the tool, prompts used, facts checked, changes made, and remaining uncertainty.
  10. Save the assignment as ai-fundamentals-Lesson-1.txt.

Screen-reader help: JAWS and NVDA users can press H for headings, B for buttons, and E for edit fields in browse mode. Narrator users can navigate with Scan Mode. Press Tab and Shift+Tab for interactive controls. Because AI interfaces change frequently, listen to each control’s current accessible name.

What should happen: You can complete the procedure and verify the result using the keyboard, your screen reader, or your braille display.

If that does not happen: Confirm focus is in the intended edit field or control. Review the page by headings and buttons, shorten the request, and never solve an interface problem by entering private information or starting a paid trial.

Practice: Explain three appropriate AI uses and three tasks that still require human judgment.
