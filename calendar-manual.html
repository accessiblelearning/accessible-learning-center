Google: Use Google Calendar with a screen reader

Purpose: This manual teaches Google Calendar from the beginning to a person using a screen reader. It explains exactly what to press, which Calendar area or event field should receive focus, what should be announced, and how to recover when the result is different.

Reference setup: This manual focuses on Google Calendar on the web in a current Chrome browser, with practical iPhone/iPad and Android sections.

Screen reader recommendation: Google recommends using Calendar as a web application. JAWS users should generally turn the Virtual PC Cursor off, NVDA users should use Focus Mode, VoiceOver users should turn Quick Nav off, and Narrator users may need Scan Mode off so Calendar receives its own shortcuts.

How to Use This Manual

  • Work through Parts 1-14 before concentrating on your screen reader. Those sections teach Calendar itself.

  • Google recommends Schedule view, also called Agenda view, for screen-reader users. Press a or 5.

  • Press c to create a normal event.

  • Press Shift+c to open the create-type dialog for Event, Out of office, Task, and other available types.

  • Press t to return to today.

  • Press / to search Calendar.

  • Press g to go to a specific date.

  • Press Ctrl+/ on Windows or Command+/ on Mac to review Google's current Calendar shortcuts.

  • Inside an open event, Alt+1 through Alt+7 on Windows can announce specific event information without moving focus.

  • Before sending an invitation, verify the guest address, event date/time, time zone, and meeting location/link.

Part 1 - What Google Calendar Is

Google Calendar is a scheduling application for appointments, meetings, invitations, tasks, working-location information, and shared calendars. It is part of Google Workspace and integrates with Gmail, Google Meet, Google Tasks, and Google Contacts.

  • Event: an appointment or meeting on a date/time.

  • All-day event: an event without a specific time block.

  • Guest: a person invited to an event.

  • Organizer: the person/account that owns the event.

  • Calendar: one collection of events. One account can display several calendars.

  • Schedule view: chronological list of upcoming events; Google recommends this view for screen readers.

  • Main drawer: left-side area with Create, small calendar, and calendar lists.

  • Events area: the main calendar view.

  • Banner area: search, settings, view switcher, account, and other controls.

  • Right sidebar: Tasks, Keep, Contacts, and other side-panel apps when available.

Part 2 - Open Calendar and Confirm the Account

2.1 Open Google Calendar

What you will learn: Reach Calendar and verify the correct Google account.

Before you begin: Use a supported current browser.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open Chrome.

  2. Press Ctrl+L.

  3. Type calendar.google.com.

  4. Press Enter.

  5. Wait for Calendar to load.

  6. Use Tab/F6 or screen-reader navigation to locate the Google account/profile control.

  7. Confirm the correct name or email address before creating or sharing events.

  8. Use the Skip to main content button if it is offered near the top.

What should happen: Google Calendar opens under the intended account.

If that does not happen: If the wrong account is active, switch accounts before creating events or inviting people.

Practice: Open Calendar and confirm the account.

Part 3 - Learn the Calendar Interface

Google documents four major Calendar areas:

  • Banner area: main drawer toggle, search, Help, Settings, view switcher, Google apps, and account.

  • Events area: day/week/month/year/schedule content.

  • Main drawer area: Create, small calendar, people search, and calendar list.

  • Right sidebar: Google apps such as Tasks, Keep, and Contacts.

3.1 Move Between Calendar Areas

What you will learn: Recognize which part of Calendar currently has focus.

Before you begin: Calendar is open.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press Tab repeatedly from the top of the page and listen to the major controls.

  2. Use Shift+Tab to move backward.

  3. Press a to switch to Schedule view and place focus in the event-oriented view.

  4. Press Ctrl+Alt+period on Windows to move toward the right sidebar when it is available.

  5. Press Escape to close a menu/search and return toward the main Calendar interface.

What should happen: You can tell whether focus is in Banner, Events, Main drawer, or Right sidebar.

If that does not happen: If single-letter shortcuts type into a field, press Escape and ensure your screen reader is in web-application/focus mode.

Practice: Identify all four areas.

Part 4 - Turn On and Review Keyboard Shortcuts

4.1 Enable Calendar Shortcuts in Settings

What you will learn: Make Calendar single-letter commands available.

Before you begin: Calendar is open.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press s to open Settings if shortcuts are already active. If not, navigate to Settings with Tab/menus.

  2. Find the Keyboard shortcuts setting.

  3. Turn Enable keyboard shortcuts on.

  4. Save if the page presents a Save control.

  5. Return to Calendar.

  6. Press Ctrl+/ to open the shortcut list.

What should happen: Calendar's documented keyboard shortcuts are available.

If that does not happen: If c, t, a, or other shortcuts do nothing, verify this setting and the screen-reader application mode.

Practice: Enable shortcuts and open the shortcut list.

Current help shortcut: Google documents Ctrl+/ on Windows/ChromeOS and Command+/ on macOS to review Calendar shortcuts. Some older references use question mark; use Google's current shortcut list as the final reference.

6.1 Review Upcoming Events

What you will learn: Move chronologically through upcoming events.

Before you begin: Press a to enter Schedule view.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press Up/Down Arrow to move among events on the current date.

  2. Listen for event title and time information.

  3. Press Left/Right Arrow to move to another day/date grouping as available.

  4. Press n to shift forward.

  5. Press p to shift backward.

  6. Press Enter on an event to open its details.

What should happen: You can move event-by-event without scanning a visual grid.

If that does not happen: If Up/Down Arrow does not move among events, the current day may have no events or focus may not be in the Events area.

Practice: Review five upcoming events or dates.

Part 7 - Navigate Day, Week, Month, and Year Views

7.1 Day View

  • Press d or 1.

  • Use Up/Down Arrow to move between the all-day section and regular-events section when events exist.

  • Use Tab to move between events in the active section.

  • Press n for next day and p for previous day.

7.2 Week View

  • Press w or 2.

  • Use Left/Right Arrow between days of the week.

  • Use Up/Down Arrow between all-day and timed-event areas.

  • Use Tab among events.

  • Press n or p to scroll next/previous week; press w again to move focus to the beginning of the displayed week when needed.

7.3 Month View

  • Press m or 3.

  • Use Left/Right Arrow between days and Up/Down Arrow between weeks.

  • Use Tab through events.

  • If a day has hidden extra events, move to the More button and press Enter/Space.

  • Press n or p for next/previous month; press m to place focus at the beginning of the displayed month when needed.

7.4 Year View

  • Press y or 6.

  • Use Left/Right Arrow between days.

  • Use Up/Down Arrow to the same weekday in previous/next weeks.

  • Press Enter to open a dialog listing the selected day's events.

  • Press Space to enter Month view for the selected date.

  • Press n or p for next/previous year.

Part 8 - Go to Today and Go to a Specific Date

8.1 Return to Today

What you will learn: Recover quickly when you have navigated far away.

Before you begin: Calendar has keyboard shortcuts enabled.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press t.

  2. Listen for the displayed period to return to today.

  3. Press a or d if you need focus placed directly on today's events/date.

What should happen: Calendar returns to the current date.

If that does not happen: If the display changes but focus seems elsewhere, press a for Schedule view.

Practice: Navigate several months away and return with t.

8.2 Go to a Specific Date

What you will learn: Jump to a date without stepping day by day.

Before you begin: Know the target date.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press g.

  2. Type the date in the format expected by your locale, for example 09/15/2026.

  3. Press Enter.

  4. If you are in Week/Month/Year view and focus does not land directly on the date, press a or d.

What should happen: Calendar displays the period containing that date.

If that does not happen: If the date is rejected, check locale/date format.

Practice: Go to three known dates.

Part 9 - Create a Basic Event

9.1 Create an Event with the c Shortcut

What you will learn: Create a complete timed event.

Before you begin: Keyboard shortcuts are active.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press c.

  2. Wait for the event details page.

  3. Type a clear event title in the initial title field.

  4. Press Tab to the date and time controls.

  5. Enter the start date.

  6. Enter the start time.

  7. Enter the end date/time.

  8. Use the All day checkbox only when there is truly no specific time.

  9. Tab through location, conferencing, notifications, and description as needed.

  10. Review the title, date, time, time zone, and calendar.

  11. Press Ctrl+S or Ctrl+Enter on Windows/ChromeOS, or use the Save button.

What should happen: The event appears on the selected calendar.

If that does not happen: If Save asks whether to send invitations, review guest changes before confirming.

Practice: Create a 30-minute practice event.

Time-zone habit: When scheduling across locations, verify the event time zone rather than assuming everyone's local time is the same.

Part 10 - Invite Guests

10.1 Add Guests to an Event

What you will learn: Invite the correct people and avoid sending to the wrong address.

Before you begin: Create a new event or press e on an existing event.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press Tab until the Guests edit field is announced.

  2. Type the guest's name or email address.

  3. Listen to the suggestion.

  4. Press Enter only on the correct person.

  5. Use Down Arrow through additional suggestions when necessary.

  6. Repeat for more guests.

  7. Move through the invited-guest list and verify addresses.

  8. Complete the event and save.

  9. If Calendar asks whether to send invitation emails, choose the intended option.

What should happen: Guests are added and receive invitations when invitations are sent.

If that does not happen: If similar names appear, type more of the exact email address before selecting.

Practice: Add a safe test guest and inspect the invited-guest list before cancelling or saving.

10.2 Mark a Guest Optional

What you will learn: Indicate that attendance is not required.

Before you begin: Edit an event containing guests.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Tab to the invited-guest list.

  2. Use Arrow keys to the desired guest.

  3. Navigate to the optional-attendance control for that guest.

  4. Activate it.

  5. Save the event.

What should happen: The guest remains invited but is marked optional.

If that does not happen: If the optional control is not available, your Calendar edition or event type may not support it.

Practice: Inspect the control on a practice event.

Part 11 - Create Recurring Events

11.1 Create a Weekly Recurring Event

What you will learn: Repeat an event automatically.

Before you begin: Create a new event or edit an existing one.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Tab to the Does not repeat drop-down.

  2. Press Enter or Space.

  3. Use Arrow keys to a preset such as Weekly.

  4. Press Enter/Space.

  5. Review the recurrence summary.

  6. Save the event.

What should happen: Calendar creates a recurring series.

If that does not happen: If the wrong recurrence is selected, reopen the recurrence menu before saving.

Practice: Create a weekly event and then delete the test series.

11.2 Create a Custom Recurrence

What you will learn: Control frequency, days, and end date.

Before you begin: Open the recurrence menu.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Choose Custom.

  2. Set the repeat interval.

  3. Tab through day-of-week choices and press Space on the intended days.

  4. Choose an end condition/date if needed.

  5. Move to Done and press Enter/Space.

  6. Review the recurrence summary before saving.

What should happen: The event follows the custom recurrence rule.

If that does not happen: If the rule is complicated, read the summary carefully before saving to avoid creating hundreds of unintended events.

Practice: Create an every-other-week test recurrence.

Part 12 - Edit, Review, and Delete Events

12.1 Review an Event Without Moving Through Every Field

What you will learn: Use Google's event-field announcement shortcuts.

Before you begin: Navigate to an event and press Enter to open details.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press Alt+1 on Windows to hear the title.

  2. Press Alt+2 to hear date/time.

  3. Press Alt+3 to hear guests.

  4. Press Alt+4 to hear rooms/location.

  5. Press Alt+5 to hear description.

  6. Press Alt+6 to hear attachments.

  7. Press Alt+7 to hear notifications.

  8. Press Escape when finished.

What should happen: Calendar announces the requested information while focus remains stable.

If that does not happen: If some information is not announced, temporarily use your screen reader's webpage-reading mode to inspect additional event content.

Practice: Review all seven fields on a practice event.

12.2 Edit an Event

What you will learn: Change event details intentionally.

Before you begin: Navigate to the event in Calendar view.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press e.

  2. Tab to the field you want to change.

  3. Make the change.

  4. Review date/time and guests again.

  5. Press Ctrl+S/Ctrl+Enter or activate Save.

  6. If it is a recurring event, choose whether the change applies to one event or the series.

What should happen: The selected event details change.

If that does not happen: For recurring events, read the scope choice carefully; changing the entire series can alter many dates.

Practice: Edit one practice event title and restore it.

12.3 Delete an Event

What you will learn: Remove an event deliberately.

Before you begin: Navigate to a disposable practice event.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Read the event title again.

  2. Press Delete or Backspace on Windows/ChromeOS.

  3. If prompted about recurrence, choose the correct scope.

  4. If prompted about notifying guests, make the intended choice.

  5. Use z immediately if you need to undo a recent deletion.

What should happen: The event is removed from the calendar scope you selected.

If that does not happen: If the wrong recurring scope is selected, undo immediately if available.

Practice: Delete and undo one practice event.

13.1 RSVP from the Calendar Events View

What you will learn: Respond to an invitation without opening Gmail.

Before you begin: Navigate to the invitation event.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open the context menu with Shift+F10 or the Application key on Windows.

  2. Use y, n, or m when the menu offers those shortcuts, or Arrow to Yes/No/Maybe and press Enter.

  3. Listen for confirmation.

  4. Open the event if you want to verify your response.

What should happen: The organizer receives your RSVP status.

If that does not happen: If the context menu does not expose RSVP choices, press Enter on the event and use the response controls inside the event.

Practice: Respond to a safe invitation or inspect the menu without changing the response.

Forwarded invitation caution: Google warns that forwarding an invitation can allow the recipient to respond, invite others if permissions permit, and potentially modify the forwarder's RSVP. Forward invitations only when appropriate.

13.2 Propose a New Time

What you will learn: Suggest another time when the organizer allows it.

Before you begin: Open an invitation you cannot attend.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open the response options near Maybe.

  2. Choose Propose a new time.

  3. Enter or choose the alternative date/time.

  4. Add a short note if useful.

  5. Send the proposal.

What should happen: The organizer receives a proposed alternate time.

If that does not happen: Google does not allow proposing a new time for certain events such as all-day events or very large guest lists.

Practice: Inspect the Propose a new time control on a suitable invitation.

Part 14 - Search Calendar

14.1 Perform a Simple Search

What you will learn: Find an event by title, person, location, or keyword.

Before you begin: Calendar is open.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press /.

  2. Type the search term.

  3. Press Enter.

  4. Use Arrow keys through the results.

  5. Press Enter on the desired event.

  6. Press Escape to close event details/search as needed.

What should happen: Calendar shows matching events.

If that does not happen: If results are broad, use advanced search options.

Practice: Search for a known event title.

14.2 Use Advanced Search

What you will learn: Limit Calendar search using fields.

Before you begin: Begin from the Calendar search box.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open the search-options form.

  2. Tab through filters such as calendars, keywords, participant, location, excluded words, and date range.

  3. Fill only the filters that matter.

  4. Run the search.

  5. Use Arrow keys through results.

What should happen: Search results are narrowed by the selected criteria.

If that does not happen: If no results appear, remove one filter and search again.

Practice: Search by participant and date range.

Part 15 - Notifications and Reminders

15.1 Set Notifications on a New Event

What you will learn: Control how and when you are reminded.

Before you begin: Create or edit an event.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Tab to the notification method/time controls.

  2. Choose Notification or Email when offered.

  3. Set the time before the event.

  4. Use Add notification if you want another reminder.

  5. Remove unneeded notifications.

  6. Save the event.

What should happen: Calendar uses the selected reminder methods/times.

If that does not happen: If system notifications never appear, check both Calendar settings and browser/Windows notification permissions.

Practice: Create an event with two different reminder times.

Accessibility: If you cannot rely on sound, configure reminders that you can perceive visually, through vibration on mobile, email, or another accessible notification channel.

Part 16 - Google Meet and Video Meetings

What you will learn: Create a video-conference link for invitees.

Before you begin: Create/edit an event.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Tab to Add Google Meet video conferencing or the conferencing control.

16.2 Join a Video Meeting from Calendar

What you will learn: Open the correct meeting from its event.

Before you begin: Navigate to the event shortly before start time.

Exactly how to do it

  3. Press Enter.

  4. Wait for Google Meet to open in a new tab.

  5. Use the Google Meet accessibility workflow to configure microphone/camera and join.

What should happen: The meeting page opens.

If that does not happen: If the event has more than one conferencing option, verify which link you are activating.

Practice: Open a safe test Meet event without joining if appropriate.

Part 17 - Find a Time and Suggested Times

17.1 Use Suggested Times

What you will learn: Let Calendar find times when guests are available.

Before you begin: Create/edit an event and add guests.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Tab to Suggested times.

  2. Press Enter or Down Arrow to open the menu.

  3. Use Arrow keys through suggestions.

  4. Press Enter on an acceptable time.

  5. Review the updated date/time fields.

  6. Save.

What should happen: The event time updates to the selected suggested time.

If that does not happen: If no suggestions appear, guest calendars may not expose availability or there may be no common free slot.

Practice: Inspect suggestions on a practice meeting.

17.2 Use Find a Time

What you will learn: Explore guest availability more directly.

Before you begin: Create/edit an event and add at least one guest.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Tab to the Event Details tablist.

  2. Use Right Arrow to Find a Time.

  3. Press Tab into the calendar panel.

  4. Use Arrow keys to review available times.

  5. Press Enter on the desired time.

  6. Review the event date/time.

  7. Save.

What should happen: The event changes to the selected time.

If that does not happen: If Find a Time is unavailable, your account type or guest setup may not support it.

Practice: Use Find a Time on a test event.

Part 19 - Share a Calendar

19.1 Share a Calendar with a Person or Group

What you will learn: Give someone controlled access to one calendar.

Before you begin: Share only a calendar you own or are authorized to manage.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open Calendar Settings.

  2. Move to Settings for my calendars.

  3. Choose the calendar.

  4. Find Share with specific people or groups / Shared with.

  5. Choose Add people and groups.

  6. Enter the person's/group's email address.

  7. Choose the permission level.

  8. Review the recipient and permission.

  9. Send/save.

What should happen: The person receives access to the calendar at the selected level.

If that does not happen: Work/school administrators can restrict sharing, especially external sharing.

Practice: Open sharing settings on a non-sensitive calendar and inspect permission levels.

Sharing safety: Calendar sharing can reveal event titles, times, locations, guest lists, and details depending on the selected permission. Use the least access necessary.

Part 20 - Manage Multiple Calendars

20.2 Create an Event on the Correct Calendar

  3. Choose the intended calendar.

  4. Review the calendar name before saving.

  5. Complete and save the event.

What should happen: The event belongs to the selected calendar.

If that does not happen: Moving an event later may be restricted if you do not own both calendars.

Practice: Create a practice event and verify the calendar field.

Part 22 - JAWS with Google Calendar

Google recommends Calendar as a web application with the JAWS Virtual PC Cursor off for normal calendar navigation.

22.1 Prepare JAWS for Calendar

What you will learn: Let Calendar receive its own single-letter shortcuts.

Before you begin: Calendar is open with JAWS.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press JAWS+Z.

  2. Listen for Virtual PC Cursor off.

  3. Press a to enter Schedule view.

  4. Use Up/Down Arrow through events.

Practice: Navigate Schedule view and create/cancel one test event.

Temporary review mode: Google notes that some event details may require screen-reader browse/virtual mode. When an event is open, you can temporarily turn the Virtual PC Cursor on to inspect non-tabbable content, then turn it off again before returning to Calendar shortcuts.

Part 25 - VoiceOver with Google Calendar

25.1 Prepare VoiceOver for Calendar

What you will learn: Let Calendar receive application shortcuts on Mac.

Before you begin: Calendar is open with VoiceOver.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press Left Arrow and Right Arrow together until Quick Nav is off.

  2. Press a for Schedule view.

  3. Use Arrow keys through events.

  4. Press c to create an event.

  5. Press Escape to cancel.

What should happen: Calendar shortcuts work and VoiceOver follows focus.

If that does not happen: If VoiceOver intercepts letter/arrow navigation, confirm Quick Nav is off.

Practice: Move through Schedule view and open/cancel one event.

Review mode: For event details that are not tabbable, Google recommends temporarily turning Quick Nav on to use VoiceOver webpage navigation, then turning it off before resuming Calendar shortcuts.

Part 28 - Security and Privacy

  • Verify the active Google account before creating or sharing calendar data.

  • Event descriptions can contain confidential information; do not assume only the organizer can see them.

  • Guest lists can reveal personal or organizational relationships.

  • Broad calendar sharing can reveal titles, locations, and schedule patterns.

  • Do not join unexpected meeting links without verifying the organizer/event.

  • Be cautious with forwarded invitations and unfamiliar calendar subscriptions.

  • Use the least-powerful sharing permission that meets the need.

  • Work/school calendars may be governed by organization retention and sharing policies.

28.1 Inspect an Unexpected Invitation

What you will learn: Decide whether an unfamiliar event is legitimate before interacting.

Before you begin: An unexpected invitation appears.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Read the organizer name/address.

  2. Review the event title, date/time, guests, description, and meeting link using event review shortcuts.

  3. Do not open unfamiliar attachments/links yet.

  4. Verify the organizer through a trusted channel if necessary.

  5. Respond or remove/report only after verifying.

What should happen: You make a deliberate trust decision before clicking meeting content.

If that does not happen: If the event is clearly malicious or spam, use available reporting/removal controls and avoid the attached links.

Practice: Review a harmless invitation as though it were unexpected.

Part 29 - Troubleshooting

29.1 Single-Letter Calendar Shortcuts Do Not Work

What you will learn: Restore Calendar keyboard shortcuts and application mode.

Before you begin: Calendar is open.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press Escape to leave any text field/menu.

  2. Verify keyboard shortcuts are enabled in Calendar Settings.

  3. JAWS: turn Virtual PC Cursor off.

  4. NVDA: enter Focus Mode.

  5. Narrator: turn Scan Mode off.

  6. VoiceOver: turn Quick Nav off.

  7. Press a.

  8. Press t.

What should happen: Calendar responds to Schedule-view and Today commands.

If that does not happen: If shortcuts still fail, refresh the page and review Ctrl+/ shortcut help.

Practice: Memorize this recovery sequence.

29.2 You Cannot Find an Event

What you will learn: Use Search and date navigation before assuming the event is gone.

Before you begin: Know at least one clue.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press /.

  2. Search by title, participant, or location.

  3. If you know the date, press g and enter it.

  4. Press a to review the date in Schedule view.

  5. Check that the relevant calendar is visible.

What should happen: You determine whether the event exists and which calendar contains it.

If that does not happen: If a calendar is hidden, its event may not appear in combined views.

Practice: Find one event by search and one by date.

29.3 You Created an Event at the Wrong Time

What you will learn: Check time zone and edit the event.

Before you begin: Open the event.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press Alt+2 to review date/time.

  2. Press e.

  3. Review the time zone control.

  4. Correct the start/end time or time zone.

  5. Save.

  6. If guests are affected, choose whether to send an update.

What should happen: The event displays at the intended time.

If that does not happen: If the event belongs to another organizer, you may not have permission to change its time.

Practice: Create a test event with a second time zone and inspect it.

29.4 A Recurring Event Changed Too Many Dates

What you will learn: Check series scope and undo quickly.

Before you begin: A recent recurring edit affected more events than intended.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Press z if the change is still undoable.

  2. Open one event in the series.

  3. Press e.

  4. Make the intended change.

  5. When Calendar asks for the recurrence scope, choose This event only, This and following events, or All events carefully.

  6. Save.

What should happen: Only the intended occurrences are affected.

If that does not happen: If Undo is no longer available, manually correct the series or individual events.

Practice: Practice on a disposable recurrence.

29.5 You Cannot Share or Add a Room

What you will learn: Check Workspace permissions/admin restrictions.

Before you begin: A sharing/room feature is missing or unavailable.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Confirm whether this is a personal or work/school account.

  2. Review your permission level on the calendar.

  3. Recognize that admins can restrict external sharing and room resources.

  4. Contact the calendar owner/admin if access is needed.

What should happen: You identify a permissions/edition limitation rather than repeatedly retrying the same control.

If that does not happen: Do not duplicate calendars or circumvent organizational rules.

Practice: Inspect permissions on a shared calendar.
