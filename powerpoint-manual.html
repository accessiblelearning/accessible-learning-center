About this manual

This manual targets PowerPoint in Microsoft 365 desktop on Windows. PowerPoint contains several work areas, including the thumbnails pane, slide editing area, notes pane, and ribbon. Press F6 to move among these areas and Tab or Shift+Tab to move among objects on a slide.

Use built-in slide layouts whenever possible. Layout placeholders provide structure that is easier to navigate and maintain than manually positioned text boxes. Give every slide a unique, descriptive title.

Accessible slide practices

  • Use built-in slide layouts and give every slide a unique title.
  • Keep text concise and use real bulleted or numbered lists.
  • Maintain strong contrast and do not use color as the only way to communicate meaning.
  • Add concise alternative text to meaningful pictures, charts, shapes, and other visual objects.
  • Mark objects decorative only when they add no information.
  • Use the Reading Order pane to arrange objects logically, with the title before related content.
  • Use simple tables with a designated header row. Avoid merged or split cells.
  • For charts, retain source values and add a plain-text summary of the important result.
  • Use meaningful link text that identifies the destination.
  • Add speaker-note prompts to describe visuals aloud during a presentation.
  • Avoid flashing content and unnecessary animation.
  • Run Check Accessibility and test the entire deck with the keyboard.

Reading order

Screen readers follow the slide's object order, which may differ from the visual arrangement. Open the Reading Order pane through Alt+Q or an Accessibility Checker recommendation, then move objects into a logical sequence. Test that sequence by pressing Tab through the slide.

Images, tables, and charts

Alt text should explain why a visual matters in the presentation. It should be concise, specific, and based on the presentation context. A chart needs alt text plus access to exact source values and a short text summary. A table needs clear headers and a simple structure.

Presenting accessibly

Start the slide show with F5, move with the Arrow keys, and press Escape to finish. Speak important information shown in images, charts, and demonstrations. Do not ask the audience to identify information only by color, position, or appearance.

Ten-lesson course sequence

  1. Lesson 1: PowerPoint Basics: Slides, Placeholders, and Saving — Create a basic presentation, move among slide placeholders with the keyboard, and save the file.
  2. Lesson 2: Slide Layouts, Titles, and Organizing a Presentation — Use built-in slide layouts and meaningful titles to create a clear presentation structure.
  3. Lesson 3: Text, Bullets, and Clear Slide Content — Create concise, structured slide text that works for screen-reader users and visual audiences.
  4. Lesson 4: Images, Alternative Text, and Decorative Objects — Insert an image and make its meaning available to people who cannot see it.
  5. Lesson 5: Reading Order and Keyboard Navigation — Arrange slide objects so a screen reader encounters them in a logical order.
  6. Lesson 6: Accessible Tables and Charts — Present structured data with clear headers, accessible descriptions, and a text summary.
  7. Lesson 7: Meaningful Links and Contact Information — Add links that clearly identify their destination and work with keyboard navigation.
  8. Lesson 8: Color, Contrast, Fonts, and Accessible Design — Apply a professional design that remains readable with low vision and does not communicate by color alone.
  9. Lesson 9: Speaker Notes and Presenting with the Keyboard — Prepare concise speaker notes and deliver a presentation using keyboard commands.
  10. Lesson 10: Final Accessible Workplace Presentation — Independently create, check, and practice a professional accessible presentation.
