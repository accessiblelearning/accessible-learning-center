About this manual

This manual targets PowerPoint in Microsoft 365 desktop on Windows. PowerPoint contains several work areas, including the thumbnails pane, slide editing area, notes pane, and ribbon. Press F6 to move among these areas and Tab or Shift+Tab to move among objects on a slide.

Use built-in slide layouts whenever possible. Layout placeholders provide structure that is easier to navigate and maintain than manually positioned text boxes. Give every slide a unique, descriptive title.

Accessible slide practices

  • Use built-in slide layouts and give every slide a unique title.
  • Keep text concise and use real bulleted or numbered lists.
  • Maintain strong contrast and do not use color as the only way to communicate meaning.
  • Add concise alternative text to meaningful pictures, charts, shapes, and other visual objects.
  • Mark objects decorative only when they add no information.
  • Use the Reading Order pane to arrange objects logically, with the title before related content.
  • Use simple tables with a designated header row. Avoid merged or split cells.
  • For charts, retain source values and add a plain-text summary of the important result.
  • Use meaningful link text that identifies the destination.
  • Add speaker-note prompts to describe visuals aloud during a presentation.
  • Avoid flashing content and unnecessary animation.
  • Run Check Accessibility and test the entire deck with the keyboard.

Reading order

Screen readers follow the slide's object order, which may differ from the visual arrangement. Open the Reading Order pane through Alt+Q or an Accessibility Checker recommendation, then move objects into a logical sequence. Test that sequence by pressing Tab through the slide.

Images, tables, and charts

Alt text should explain why a visual matters in the presentation. It should be concise, specific, and based on the presentation context. A chart needs alt text plus access to exact source values and a short text summary. A table needs clear headers and a simple structure.

Presenting accessibly

Start the slide show with F5, move with the Arrow keys, and press Escape to finish. Speak important information shown in images, charts, and demonstrations. Do not ask the audience to identify information only by color, position, or appearance.

Basic procedure

1. PowerPoint Basics: Slides, Placeholders, and Saving

What you will learn: Create a basic presentation, move among slide placeholders with the keyboard, and save the file.

Before you begin: Open the application and a fictional, instructor-provided, or nonprivate practice file. Save a separate working copy before making major changes.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open Microsoft PowerPoint and create a Blank Presentation.
  2. Press F6 several times and listen as JAWS moves among the slide area, thumbnails pane, notes pane, and ribbon.
  3. Return to the slide area. Press Tab to move between the title and subtitle placeholders.
  4. Type Workplace Technology Training in the title placeholder.
  5. Move to the subtitle placeholder and type Prepared by followed by a fictional name.
  6. Press Ctrl+M to create a new slide.
  7. Type Training Goals in the title placeholder. In the content placeholder, enter Learn keyboard commands, Practice accessible documents, and Complete workplace assignments as separate lines.
  8. Press Page Up and Page Down in the thumbnails pane to review both slides.
  9. Press Ctrl+S and save the presentation as PowerPoint-Lesson-1.pptx.

Keyboard help: Press Alt+Q to search PowerPoint commands when Ribbon KeyTips differ. For JAWS help, press Insert+Spacebar, then J, and search for the task.

What should happen: You can complete the procedure and verify the result using the keyboard, your screen reader, or your braille display.

If that does not happen: Press Escape to leave an unexpected menu or dialog, confirm the active window and keyboard focus, then repeat one step at a time. Use Undo when a reversible content change occurred.

Practice: Create a two-slide workplace technology training presentation with a title slide and a Training Goals slide.

Exactly how to do it

3. Text, Bullets, and Clear Slide Content

What you will learn: Create concise, structured slide text that works for screen-reader users and visual audiences.

Before you begin: Open the application and a fictional, instructor-provided, or nonprivate practice file. Save a separate working copy before making major changes.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Create a new presentation with the title Accessible Customer Service.
  2. Add a slide titled Greeting a Customer.
  3. Enter three bullets: Introduce yourself, Ask how you can help, and Listen before responding.
  4. With focus in the second bullet, press Tab to create a sub-bullet and type Clarify the request. Press Shift+Tab if you need to return it to the main level.
  5. Add a slide titled Resolving a Request.
  6. Create a numbered list with four steps: Identify the problem, Confirm the desired result, Complete the action, and Verify satisfaction.
  7. Avoid long paragraphs. Keep each bullet to one clear idea.
  8. Use Ctrl+B only when emphasis adds meaning; do not rely on bold alone to communicate status.
  9. Review each text object by pressing Tab through the slide, then F2 or Enter to edit its text.
  10. Save the presentation as PowerPoint-Lesson-3.pptx.

Keyboard help: Press Alt+Q to search PowerPoint commands when Ribbon KeyTips differ. For JAWS help, press Insert+Spacebar, then J, and search for the task.

4. Images, Alternative Text, and Decorative Objects

What you will learn: Insert an image and make its meaning available to people who cannot see it.

Before you begin: Open the application and a fictional, instructor-provided, or nonprivate practice file. Save a separate working copy before making major changes.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open PowerPoint-Lesson-3.pptx and add a slide titled Accessible Communication.
  2. Press Alt+Q, type Insert Pictures, and choose the appropriate command.
  3. Insert a locally available, nonprivate image related to communication or technology.
  4. Press Tab until JAWS announces the picture object.
  5. Press Shift+F10 to open the context menu. Choose View Alt Text or Edit Alt Text.
  6. Write concise alt text explaining the image's purpose in the slide. Do not begin with Image of unless that wording adds meaning.
  7. If you add a purely decorative shape, mark it as decorative in the Alt Text pane when that option is available.
  8. Add a visible caption only if the audience needs additional context.
  9. Press Tab through every object and confirm that JAWS identifies it.
  10. Run Alt+Q, Check Accessibility and review image-related warnings.
  11. Save the presentation as PowerPoint-Lesson-4.pptx.

Keyboard help: Press Alt+Q to search PowerPoint commands when Ribbon KeyTips differ. For JAWS help, press Insert+Spacebar, then J, and search for the task.

5. Reading Order and Keyboard Navigation

What you will learn: Arrange slide objects so a screen reader encounters them in a logical order.

Before you begin: Open the application and a fictional, instructor-provided, or nonprivate practice file. Save a separate working copy before making major changes.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open PowerPoint-Lesson-4.pptx.
  2. Choose a slide containing a title, text, and image.
  3. Press Alt+Q, type Reading Order, and open the Reading Order pane or use the Accessibility Checker recommendation.
  4. Review the listed objects. The title should be read before the main content, and supporting objects should follow logically.
  5. Use the pane's Move Up and Move Down controls to correct the sequence if needed.
  6. Mark decorative objects decorative rather than leaving them unnamed.
  7. Close the pane and return to the slide.
  8. Press Tab repeatedly to test the object sequence. Use Shift+Tab to move backward.
  9. Repeat the test on every slide.
  10. Run Check Accessibility and resolve reading-order warnings that apply.
  11. Save the presentation as PowerPoint-Lesson-5.pptx.

Keyboard help: Press Alt+Q to search PowerPoint commands when Ribbon KeyTips differ. For JAWS help, press Insert+Spacebar, then J, and search for the task.

If that does not happen: Press Escape to leave an unexpected menu or dialog, confirm the active window and keyboard focus, then repeat one step at a time. Use Undo when a reversible content change occurred.

Practice: Review and correct the reading order of every slide in the customer-service presentation.

6. Accessible Tables and Charts

What you will learn: Present structured data with clear headers, accessible descriptions, and a text summary.

Before you begin: Open the application and a fictional, instructor-provided, or nonprivate practice file. Save a separate working copy before making major changes.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Create a presentation titled Monthly Training Report.
  2. Add a slide titled Training Completion Table.
  3. Use Alt+Q to find Insert Table. Create a table with three columns and five rows.
  4. Enter the headers Month, Enrolled, and Completed in the first row.
  5. Enter fictional data for January through April.
  6. Confirm that the first row is designated as a header row through Table Design options.
  7. Add a slide titled Completion Trend.
  8. Create a simple chart from the same data using Insert Chart and its data worksheet.
  9. Add alt text describing the chart's purpose and main trend.
  10. Keep the table slide so exact values remain available.
  11. Add a short text statement on the chart slide summarizing the trend without relying on color.
  12. Run Check Accessibility and review the table and chart.
  13. Save the presentation as PowerPoint-Lesson-6.pptx.

7. Meaningful Links and Contact Information

What you will learn: Add links that clearly identify their destination and work with keyboard navigation.

Before you begin: Open the application and a fictional, instructor-provided, or nonprivate practice file. Save a separate working copy before making major changes.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Create a presentation titled New Employee Resources.
  2. Add a slide titled Technology Resources.
  3. Type Microsoft Accessibility Help, select the text, and press Ctrl+K.
  4. Enter https://support.microsoft.com/accessibility as the address and confirm the dialog.
  5. Add the meaningful link text Freedom Scientific JAWS Training and link it to https://www.freedomscientific.com/training/jaws/.
  6. Add a slide titled Department Contacts.
  7. Enter a fictional contact name and email address. Use Ctrl+K to create a mailto link if appropriate.
  8. Avoid link text such as click here or read more because it does not identify the destination.
  9. Press Tab through the slide objects and listen to each link with JAWS.
  10. Use Shift+F10 and Edit Link if a destination needs correction.
  11. Run Check Accessibility and review link-related suggestions.

8. Color, Contrast, Fonts, and Accessible Design

What you will learn: Apply a professional design that remains readable with low vision and does not communicate by color alone.

Before you begin: Open the application and a fictional, instructor-provided, or nonprivate practice file. Save a separate working copy before making major changes.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open PowerPoint-Lesson-7.pptx.
  2. Use Alt+Q to search for Themes and choose a simple built-in theme.
  3. Keep body text large enough to read from a distance. Use approximately 18 points or larger for body text and larger sizes for titles when the layout allows.
  4. Keep strong contrast between text and its background.
  5. Do not place text over a busy image unless a solid background makes it readable.
  6. If a status is shown in red, yellow, or green, also write words such as Needs Action, In Progress, or Complete.
  7. Avoid rapidly flashing content and excessive animations.
  8. Keep the same typeface and heading treatment across slides.
  9. Review each slide at increased zoom and with JAWS object navigation.
  10. Run Check Accessibility and fix contrast or color-use warnings when available.
  11. Save the presentation as PowerPoint-Lesson-8.pptx.

Keyboard help: Press Alt+Q to search PowerPoint commands when Ribbon KeyTips differ. For JAWS help, press Insert+Spacebar, then J, and search for the task.

What should happen: You can complete the procedure and verify the result using the keyboard, your screen reader, or your braille display.

If that does not happen: Press Escape to leave an unexpected menu or dialog, confirm the active window and keyboard focus, then repeat one step at a time. Use Undo when a reversible content change occurred.

Practice: Apply a consistent, high-contrast design to the employee-resources presentation and ensure no meaning depends on color alone.

9. Speaker Notes and Presenting with the Keyboard

What you will learn: Prepare concise speaker notes and deliver a presentation using keyboard commands.

Before you begin: Open the application and a fictional, instructor-provided, or nonprivate practice file. Save a separate working copy before making major changes.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Open PowerPoint-Lesson-8.pptx.
  2. Press F6 until JAWS announces the Notes pane for the current slide.
  3. Add two or three short speaker-note prompts. Do not paste the entire slide into the notes.
  4. Add notes to every content slide.
  5. For slides with meaningful images or charts, include a prompt to describe the important information aloud.
  6. Press F5 to start the slide show from the beginning.
  7. Use Right Arrow, Spacebar, or Page Down to move forward.
  8. Use Left Arrow, Backspace, or Page Up to move backward.
  9. Press a slide number followed by Enter to move directly to that slide when needed.
  10. Press B to display a black screen during a pause, then press B again to return.
  11. Press Escape to end the slide show.
  12. Save the presentation as PowerPoint-Lesson-9.pptx.

Keyboard help: Press Alt+Q to search PowerPoint commands when Ribbon KeyTips differ. For JAWS help, press Insert+Spacebar, then J, and search for the task.

What should happen: You can complete the procedure and verify the result using the keyboard, your screen reader, or your braille display.

If that does not happen: Press Escape to leave an unexpected menu or dialog, confirm the active window and keyboard focus, then repeat one step at a time. Use Undo when a reversible content change occurred.

Practice: Add useful speaker notes to every content slide and practice delivering the presentation entirely with the keyboard.

10. Final Accessible Workplace Presentation

What you will learn: Independently create, check, and practice a professional accessible presentation.

Before you begin: Open the application and a fictional, instructor-provided, or nonprivate practice file. Save a separate working copy before making major changes.

Exactly how to do it

  1. Create a presentation for a fictional new-employee orientation.
  2. Include a title slide and at least six additional slides.
  3. Cover Welcome, Daily Schedule, Workplace Expectations, Technology Resources, Safety or Emergency Information, and Contact Information.
  4. Use built-in layouts and give every slide a unique title.
  5. Include at least one meaningful image with alt text.
  6. Include either an accessible table or a chart with alt text, source values, and a text summary.
  7. Include at least one meaningful hyperlink.
  8. Check the reading order on every slide.
  9. Use strong contrast and do not communicate by color alone.
  10. Add concise speaker notes to every content slide.
  11. Run Check Accessibility and resolve applicable errors and warnings.
  12. Present the entire deck using only the keyboard.
  13. Save the presentation as PowerPoint-Lesson-10-Final.pptx.

Keyboard help: Press Alt+Q to search PowerPoint commands when Ribbon KeyTips differ. For JAWS help, press Insert+Spacebar, then J, and search for the task.

What should happen: You can complete the procedure and verify the result using the keyboard, your screen reader, or your braille display.

If that does not happen: Press Escape to leave an unexpected menu or dialog, confirm the active window and keyboard focus, then repeat one step at a time. Use Undo when a reversible content change occurred.

Practice: Create a professional, accessible new-employee orientation presentation with at least seven slides and all required accessibility features.
